## The edit

`base.json` with the dated `sibling` exclusion's `date` moved two hours later, from `2026-10-09T17:00:00-06:00` to `2026-10-09T19:00:00-06:00`, which is one hour after `task.generatedAt`.

## What the record now claims

The record drops a whole file out of every figure it reports, on the evidence of a commit authored after the work closed. The exclusion's relation is `sibling`, which claims the finished file is byte-identical to a copy on a branch outside this generation's line of descent and infers that the content came FROM that branch. Dated after the work, the inference runs backwards: the branch holds a copy OF this work, and the record refuses to credit a file the person did write. This is `rival-not-after-final.json` one level up and it is the more expensive of the two, because an exclusion moves `stats.finalChars` and every percentage built on it rather than one span — on this repository's own history one exclusion removed 60,616 characters, 84.7% of that run's headline survival figure. The edit is two hours and not two days for the reason the span-level one is: `2026-10-09T19:00:00-06:00` is `2026-10-10T01:00:00Z`, after the horizon, while its text still sorts before `2026-10-10T00:00:00.000Z`. A gate comparing strings would pass this file.

- Bound: no path the record excludes appears among its classified files; every exclusion names a commit and a positive character count; and an exclusion that states its commit's author date states one that can be ordered, with a `sibling` exclusion's date no later than the record's own `task.generatedAt`. The first clause is the same-set arithmetic this module exists for: `exclusions` and `files` partition the paths the run was willing to read, so a path in both means the refusal was computed and then not applied, and the record simultaneously claims the file is an import and sells labels over its spans. The second catches an exclusion that cannot be reconciled against the figures it moved — `stats.finalChars` plus the excluded characters is the size of every path the run read, and an entry with no number or no commit breaks that sum silently. The third is `RIVAL_NOT_AFTER_FINAL` one level up, added 2026-10-15, and it is the more expensive of the two to get wrong: a span-level mistake moves one span, while an exclusion drops a whole file out of every figure in the record, and on this repository's own history one exclusion removed 60,616 characters, 84.7% of that run's headline survival figure. A `sibling` exclusion asserts the finished file is byte-identical to a copy on a branch that is neither an ancestor nor a descendant of the generation; if that branch's commit was authored after the work closed then the copy is a copy OF this work rather than a source for it, the refusal is backwards, and the record drops a file the person did write. `pre_existing` and `declared` are exempt from the ordering and reported without a time check, for the reasons `RIVAL_NOT_AFTER_FINAL` exempts the relations the graph vouches for: a `pre_existing` match is an ancestor of the generation whatever a rebase did to its author date, and a `declared` import names a commit in ANOTHER repository, whose clock is not comparable to this one's. An unreadable date fails on every relation including the exempt ones, because a field that reads as provenance everywhere it is printed may not state a time nothing can order, and omitting it was always allowed. An ABSENT date is legal and unchecked: every record written before 2026-10-15 has exclusions with no date, and `vendoredPaths` in src/vendored.ts omits the field rather than writing an empty string when a clone cannot answer. A fourth clause, added 2026-11-09 with the `over_size_ceiling` reason: a refusal and an import may not wear each other's clothes, and a stated refusal must be re-derivable from the record alone. `reason: 'over_size_ceiling'` carries `relation: 'refused'` and no other relation carries it; that reason carries a positive `ceiling` and a `generatedChars`, and at least one of `chars` and `generatedChars` is strictly greater than `ceiling`; and no other reason carries `ceiling` or `generatedChars` at all. The re-derivation clause is the one with teeth beyond shape. The other three reasons point at a commit to make a claim ABOUT that commit, and a reader checks them by looking the commit up; this one is a claim about what the build was willing to read, so the only thing a reader can check is the arithmetic, and an exclusion stating a ceiling that neither of its two sizes exceeds is a file dropped from every figure in the record on a refusal that cannot have happened. `generatedChars` is required and not inferred for the asymmetric case: a path is refused if EITHER blob is over, so an agent that wrote 400,000 characters the person then cut to 200 produces `chars: 200` with the ceiling honestly exceeded on the other side, and a bound reading `chars` alone would report that honest record as the false one.
