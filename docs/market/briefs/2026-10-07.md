# Market brief — 2026-10-07

Fourth research run of the market seat. Full detail in

## Findings

1. **Four of the category's named vendors sell the same pipeline to
   both US frontier labs and Chinese AI labs, and it has nothing to do
   with equity.** A Forbes investigation (2026-08-05, by Anna Tong,
   paywalled, recorded here secondhand via an aggregator per this
   charter's rule) reported Surge AI, Mercor, AfterQuery, and Turing
   collectively sold roughly $500M/year of training data to Tencent,
   Alibaba, and ByteDance, from the same contractor pools that serve
   their US frontier-lab customers — Surge's CEO reportedly traveled
   to China to meet lab executives directly, and about 2% of Mercor's
   Q2 2026 revenue came from Chinese labs
   ([aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/forbes-surge-ai-mercor-afterquery-and-turing-sold-500myear-of-training-data-to)).
   So what: the existing neutrality argument in docs/ideas.md
   (2026-09-26) is built on equity — a lab buying into its vendor. This
   is a second, independent axis a lab buyer can worry about with zero
   equity involved: whether your data provider's pipeline also serves
   a geopolitical rival. Ursa Minor hasn't sold anything yet, so
   there's no live comparison, but nothing in CLAUDE.md commits to a
   no-dual-sale guarantee a lab brief could cite today. Ledger proposal
   below flags this as a decision only the owner can make.

2. **The category's fastest-growing vendor is now the subject of a
   detailed labor-conditions investigation.** A Gazetteer SF piece
   (2026-05-05, by Cydney Hayes, multiple anonymous former employees
   plus a December 2025 internal survey) reported punishing hours,
   abrupt terminations without severance, and no formal HR or payment
   policy at Mercor before late 2025; Mercor's spokesperson disputed or
   contextualized several specific claims
   ([Gazetteer SF](https://sf.gazetteer.co/new-troubles-at-mercor-infighting-face-time-slavery-and-inhumane-working-conditions)).
   So what: this is independent evidence of a structural cost the
   solicited-labor model carries — an assigned, managed workforce has
   to exist for the label to exist — that the outcome record's
   real-work-derived signal doesn't carry at all. A trust-narrative
   line, not a pricing one.

3. **Surge AI's newest benchmarks use language close enough to Minor's
   own pitch to risk a buyer mistaking one for the other.** Surge's
   blog confirms three new 2026 benchmarks — DAYJOB (launched
   2026-09-23, Healthcare/Finance sub-benchmarks added by 2026-10-04),
   GDP.xlsx (2026-09-30), and the umbrella "Tuesday Work Index"
   (started 2026-08-18) — explicitly framed around "economically
   valuable work" and whether an agent can "survive a 9 to 5"
   ([Surge AI blog](https://surgehq.ai/blog)). So what: these are
   still constructed, rubric-graded task sets, the exact "grader
   supplies the label" structure CLAUDE.md §1 contrasts the outcome
   record against, not real finished work joined to its own generation
   history. The risk isn't competitive overlap in substance, it's
   language collision at the exact moment a lab buyer is comparing
   pitches. Worth naming the distinction explicitly before that happens
   rather than after.

4. **No correction needed to last run's calls.** Revisited the two
   open positioning questions flagged 2026-10-05 (the "symmetric
   portability" word test and the reward-model accuracy gap) — neither
   has new public evidence this run, both stand as written.

## The gap

Unchanged in shape: no competitor, including this week's two Category
1 vendors under scrutiny, ties its signal back to whether real
finished work used the generation. What's new this week is a second
kind of gap specifically in the labor model underneath Category 1:
both the geopolitical dual-sale pattern and the labor-conditions
exposé are structural consequences of needing an assigned, managed
human workforce to produce the label at all. The outcome record has no
such workforce to manage, expose, or lose.

## For the owner

The single biggest fact this week: two of the four named vendors this
seat tracks as Ursa Minor's closest direct competitors are reported
selling the same training-data pipeline to Chinese AI labs, and one of
them is now the subject of a detailed labor-conditions investigation.
Neither is a reason to doubt Minor's bet; both are concrete, dated
evidence that the solicited-labor model carries trust costs beyond
equity capture, which last week's brief already flagged as the
category's single biggest structural risk. The one call that benefits
from being made soon, and that only you can make: whether Ursa Minor
should commit now, while it has no customers yet, to a provenance
guarantee (no dual-sale to undisclosed buyers) that the incumbents
can't retroactively match. Making that commitment before a first lab
contract exists costs nothing and can't be undone to look reactive
later.
