## What this run found

1. **An independent paper directly tests revealed preference against
   stated preference and finds revealed preference wins, which is the
   first academic source this file has found for the mechanism
   CLAUDE.md §4 argues, not just the gap it fills.** "Should I State or
   Should I Show? Aligning AI with Human Preferences" (Ellis and Huang,
   arXiv 2603.29317, 2026-03-31) runs an experiment where an AI system
   learns from a person's actual choices versus that same person's
   written description of their preferences. Choice data wins, with as
   few as two choices matching a written prompt's predictive value,
   and the gap is largest for subjects whose stated and actual
   preferences diverge most (Allais-type patterns). The sharpest
   finding for a lab pitch: subjects themselves cannot tell which
   method works better, and often pick the less accurate one. This
   file already has eight sources (2026-10-05 through 2026-10-27)
   arguing that solicited ratings and what's built on them fail. This
   is the first one arguing the positive case instead — that the
   alternative Minor already uses predicts real choices better, with a
   mechanism and a number, independent of Minor saying so about
   itself. So what: KR4.1's lab brief has had a strong negative-case
   packet for a month; it has not had a positive one until this run.
2. **A second, adjacent paper is worth citing with a sharp caveat, not
   without one.** "Future-as-Label: Scalable Supervision from
   Real-World Outcomes" (Turtel, Wilczewski, Franklin, and Skothiem,
   arXiv 2601.06336, 2026-01-09) trains a forecasting model on events'
   own eventual resolutions, no human labels at all, with a real
   result (27% Brier-score improvement, beating a larger model on
   Metaculus). It is tempting to read "real outcomes instead of human
   labels" as Minor's method in one sentence. It isn't: forecasting has
   a deterministic eventual ground truth, the same property math and
   code have and the property CLAUDE.md §1 says open-ended work lacks.
   Citing this without that distinction hands a skeptical lab
   researcher an easy objection ("that's a verifier domain, show me a
   non-verifier one"). Recorded as a second independent success case
   for the general mechanism, not as evidence Minor's own target gap
   has already been closed by someone else.
3. **Mercor's own disclosed customer concentration gives CLAUDE.md
   §4's predicted revenue shape a sourced, double-edged comp.** BigGo
   Finance (2026-07-22, citing The Information's 2026-07-09
   disclosure) reports that ~91% of Mercor's H1 2026 revenue — the
   same $614M figure this file already tracked from 2026-10-05 — came
   specifically from AI foundation-model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic,
   Google DeepMind named), calls the dependency "a sword of Damocles,"
   and states Mercor's own target of cutting that share to under
   one-third by 2030. CLAUDE.md §4 already predicts this exact shape
   for Minor ("one or two of them accounting for most of revenue...
   standard shape"), as an assumption with no comp behind it until
   now. Read one way, this validates the predicted structure as real
   and tolerated by frontier-lab buyers. Read the other way, it is the
   category's largest-by-growth vendor stating, in its own words, that
   the identical structure is dangerous enough to need a decade-long
   diversification plan — worth having on hand the next time anyone
   treats customer concentration as a hypothetical rather than a
   demonstrated lab-buyer-side risk.
