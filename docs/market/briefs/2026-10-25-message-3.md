## Findings

1. **Handshake AI's AI-training revenue has plateaued since spring,
   and its cost structure is the first real margin figure found for
   any Category 1 vendor.** The Information's exclusive puts gross
   annualized revenue at nearly $1B, up from $550M in January but
   roughly flat against the $1.1B this landscape entry already
   tracked for April — the hundredfold run-up from last year has
   stopped compounding. The same reporting discloses contractor
   costs at 60-70% of gross revenue. So what: this is the first
   concrete number this file can cite for why a solicited-labor data
   vendor's economics differ from Minor's — Minor's raw material
   carries no per-unit labor cost to generate, because it is the
   byproduct of someone doing their own real work. Added to
   positioning.md. [The Information on X](https://x.com/theinformation/status/2044105942749315090), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/handshake-s-arr-crosses-1b-as-ai-training-revenue-surges).

2. **A fifth reward-model-unreliability source goes a step further
   back than the fourth, and it is the strongest one found yet.**
   "Aligning to Illusions: Choice Blindness in Human and AI Feedback"
   (arXiv 2603.08412) finds 91% of surreptitiously swapped preferences
   go undetected by the annotators who stated them, and that at 50%
   label corruption, reward-guided Best-of-N selection performs no
   better than random while the proxy reward model still reports
   improving scores. So what: the 2026-10-24 brief already had four
   sources showing the solicited-rating pipeline is unreliable at
   every stage checked; this one shows the annotator's own
   metacognition can't even detect that a stated preference was never
   theirs. Ledger proposal below. [arXiv 2603.08412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08412).

3. **Invisible Technologies' own 2026 outlook is the clearest public
   disagreement with Ursa's no-human-grader bet found in this
   category.** Its CEO argues "2026 is about going operational, not
   going autonomous," and that human intelligence stays necessary in
   the loop "for another decade," with adoption (not model capability)
   named as the real constraint. So what: this is a named incumbent's
   public prediction running directly against CLAUDE.md §1's
   principles, worth having on file the next time a lab asks why a
   fully autonomous, grader-free pipeline is credible. [Invisible: 2026 AI trends](https://invisibletech.ai/2026-trends).

4. **The memory-interchange fragmentation this map has tracked since
   2026-10-05 is wider than this file had recorded.** Re-reading the
   original source directly turned up two more named attempts, MIF
   and memorywire, that the 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-24 notes hadn't
   carried forward from the same article. Six distinct formats have
   now launched in about a year (COGX, ByteRover, Open Memory
   Protocol, MIF, memorywire, UMP) and none has a second independent
   implementation or institutional governance. So what: the
   fragmentation is accelerating faster than any single attempt is
   gaining traction, which sharpens rather than changes this map's
   standing conclusion that nobody has closed Major's portability gap
   for the market as a whole. [stantyan.com](https://stantyan.com/blog/portable-ai-memory-or-permanent-lock-in/).
