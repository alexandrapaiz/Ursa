## What this run found

1. **An academic paper complicates, rather than confirms, the sharp
   form of "no verifier exists for open-ended domains."** "Where and
   What to Reward?" (arXiv 2605.15464, revised 2026-10-26) finds that
   training with a reward model on open-ended prompts transfers
   capability gains (including to reasoning and coding) far better
   than training with a rule-based verifier does. Read plainly: a
   reward model — the exact thing every Category 1 vendor in this
   file already sells — currently works for capability training in
   open-ended domains. So what: KR4.1's lab brief should not state the
   verifier gap as "no usable signal exists today." The paper does
   not validate the reward model's own judgments against ground
   truth, though, so it does not conflict with the eight prior
   reward-model-unreliability sources already on file — it answers "
   does training with this transfer?" and they answer "is this
   verdict correct?" The honest, still-sharp claim: reward models
   work well enough that labs keep using them, and still carry
   unmeasured, frequently-wrong judgments an outcome record's
   survival signal doesn't share. Ledger proposal below.
2. **A new Category 1 vendor, found through ordinary hiring-post
   noise rather than a lead list, blurs this file's own category
   boundary.** Cocient (new landscape.md entry, 28th) pays domain
   experts $60-130/hr to "write hard, realistic problems... grade
   model answers... identify where a model's reasoning breaks" — in
   its own words, the identical authored-task-and-rubric construction
   the rl-list.com cross-cutting note already mapped across 42 named
   companies as a separate cluster. So what: a Category 1 / "build
   the task and verifier" split that reads clean in this file's own
   structure does not hold when a single vendor markets itself as
   both. KR4.1's lab brief should state that boundary as soft, not
   structural, if it draws on it. Ledger proposal below.
3. **The same vendor also supplies a second, independent labor-pay
   data point.** Cocient's $60-130/hr brackets tightly against
   Mercor's already-tracked ~$85/hr average ($200+/hr senior). Two
   points close together is not yet a trend, but it is this file's
   second concrete figure on the labor side of a market that
   otherwise discloses nothing on the buyer side.

## The gap

Unchanged in substance, qualified by this run's own finding: the
standing Category 1 argument (no revealed-preference, outcome-joined
signal exists; every vendor sells solicited judgment on request) is
untouched. What narrows is the adjacent, easier-to-overstate claim
that no training signal at all works for open-ended domains — this
run's academic source shows one does, just not one that is accurate
the way an outcome record's survival signal is. The gap is the same
shape; the rhetoric around it needs the hedge this run adds.

## Report for the owner

The week's biggest market fact: a new academic paper shows reward
models (the exact product every Category 1 vendor sells) already
work for training open-ended capability, which means the lab-facing
pitch cannot honestly claim "no usable signal exists today" — it has
to claim the narrower, still-true thing, that the signal which exists
is frequently wrong and unmeasured, and an outcome record's survival
signal doesn't share that defect. That is a correction to how sharply
KR4.1's lab brief states its own best argument, not a reason to doubt
the argument itself. Separately, a new competitor found through
ordinary hiring-post noise (Cocient) markets itself in language that
spans two categories this file has treated as distinct, which argues
for stating that boundary as soft rather than clean the next time
anyone sizes this market in a lab-facing document. Neither finding
argues for a change to Minor's pricing posture or to the charter's own
stale $10/$30 language (still flagged and still unactioned, seven-plus
runs now, #725). The ledger proposals above and the prior runs'
unactioned items remain open and are still yours or whoever owns the
relevant surface.
