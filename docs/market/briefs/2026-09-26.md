## Findings

1. **Scale AI's customer exodus is a live proof of Ursa's central
   strategic problem.** Meta's $14.3B stake for 49% of Scale AI (June
   2025) triggered Google (~$200M/year contract), OpenAI, and Microsoft
   to cut or scale back ties, on neutrality grounds alone — they could
   not trust their training data stayed away from a competing lab's
   parent. [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4009714/metas-14-3b-stake-triggers-scale-ai-customer-exodus-could-be-a-windfall-for-rivals-like-mercor.html).
   So what: CLAUDE.md §5 already names this exact risk in the abstract;
   this is a dated, citable precedent that a lab will walk from a
   vendor for structural neutrality reasons alone, even at $200M/year
   of switching cost. Turned into a ledger proposal this run (below).

2. **Every direct preference-data competitor sells solicited judgment,
   none sells revealed preference from real finished work.** Surge,
   Scale/Outlier, Mercor, Prolific, and Invisible Technologies (all in
   landscape.md Category 1) are structurally the same shape: a person
   paid to produce a rating, ranking, or correction on request. So
   what: the outcome record's core claim (CLAUDE.md §1, "the label is
   supplied by the artifact rather than by a grader") is not yet
   contested by any competitor found this run. That's a real gap, not
   an assumed one, as of today.

3. **Human preference data is being priced out at the commodity end,
   which sharpens rather than threatens Ursa's angle.** A single piece
   of human preference data runs "$1 or higher (or even above $10 per
   prompt)" against under a penny for AI-generated feedback, yet
   frontier labs still treat human preference data as a competitive
   moat despite synthetic performing comparably in academic work.
   [rlhfbook.com](https://rlhfbook.com/c/12-synthetic-data). So what:
   the commodity RLHF-vendor market is being squeezed toward synthetic
   substitution on price; Minor should not compete there. It should
   compete on the open-ended domains (writing, research, applied
   engineering) where no rubric or synthetic grader exists at all —
   exactly where the outcome record's advantage is structural, not
   priced.

4. **No public per-unit or per-contract price exists yet for anything
   resembling an outcome-record license.** Every Category 1 vendor's
   buyer-side pricing is an undisclosed enterprise contract. The
   nearest observed comp for the *contract shape* (annual, named
   counterparty, training-relevant data) is publisher content
   licensing — OpenAI's ~$250M/5-year News Corp deal, Reddit's $203M
   disclosed in its IPO filing — but that's a different asset (bulk
   content, not outcome records). So what: nothing found this run
   argues against CLAUDE.md's six-to-seven-figure target range, but
   nothing anchors a specific number in it either. Flagged as a gap for
   next run's demand-signals ceremony, not a pricing recommendation.

5. **Ursa Major's "portable across every model" claim is untested
   against the one competitor closest to closing the user-facing gap.**
   ChatGPT's native memory (free, built in, steadily gaining per-project
   and per-chat edit controls through 2026) is closing the UX gap fast.
   It cannot close the portability gap by construction — that memory
   cannot follow a user to Claude or Gemini — but the owner should know
   the free-and-improving competitor is real and moving, not
   hypothetical.

## The gap

No competitor in any of the three categories ties its signal back to
whether real, finished work actually used the generation. Preference
vendors grade on request; arena and eval products score against a
curated or self-selected prompt set; memory layers store facts but
don't classify what happened to any specific generation. The
provenance-resolved outcome record (CLAUDE.md §1) sits in open space
this run confirms is genuinely open, not merely unclaimed because no
one has looked.

## For the owner

The single biggest fact this week: a $14B-scale acquisition just cost
Scale AI three of its largest customers purely on trust grounds, with
no product failure involved. That is the clearest real-world
confirmation available that the trust-layer bet in CLAUDE.md §0 and §5
is not a nicety, it's a demonstrated purchase criterion at the top of
the market. Nothing here argues for a pricing change today. The
pricing gap worth your attention: there is still no observed number to
anchor Minor's six-to-seven-figure target against, so the first
real disclosed RLHF-vendor contract size (not publisher content) found
in a future run should go straight to you, not just into the ledger.
