## The gap

Independent blog posts this week (agiflow.io, xtended.ai, two dev.to
posts, none citing each other) converge on the same complaint under
different names — "repetition tax," "context-switching tax," "AI
Rework Tax" — the cost of re-establishing context every time someone
switches between AI tools, estimated at 15-30 minutes per rebuild.
That is Ursa Major's value proposition stated by the market itself
rather than by this seat: users want their preferences to follow them
between tools, and today's answer is either a single-vendor memory
(ChatGPT, Claude) that doesn't travel, or a developer-facing memory
layer (Mem0, Zep) that the end user can't see or carry. Separately, a
Hacker News thread on the Universal Memory Protocol names the
identical problem from the builder side: "no standard protocol for AI
memory, each platform builds its own walled garden." The clearest
unmet need this week is the same one last several weeks have found:
the market agrees on the problem Major solves and has not converged
on who solves it. [xtended.ai: the context-switching tax](https://xtended.ai/blog/founder-productivity/context-switching-tax), [Hacker News: Universal Memory Protocol](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48428796).
