## What this run found

1. **Appen gives this file its clearest sourced "who is winning
   Category 1" claim, stated by a named incumbent rather than implied
   by a specialist's own funding round.** Appen (new landscape.md
   entry, Category 1, 24th), a 20-plus-year annotation incumbent that
   lost its anchor Google contract in January 2024, is repositioning
   around generative AI; its own H1 FY26 results show Appen China
   growing 80% on Chinese LLM developers' demand for annotated data
   while Appen Global keeps shrinking. A vendor-comparison source
   states plainly that Appen "never really owned" the frontier-RLHF
   segment and "a new generation of specialists has taken it," naming
   Surge, Mercor, and Scale, the same three names already clustered at
   the top of this file's funding and revenue figures. So what: a lab
   brief characterizing Category 1 as a market, not a list of logos,
   now has a named incumbent conceding the ground on the record,
   sharper than citing each specialist's valuation jump in isolation.
2. **Sama's documented 2023 Kenya content-moderation case names a cost
   this file's existing arguments against Category 1 have not named:
   a cost borne by the labeler, not the buyer.** Sama (new
   landscape.md entry, Category 1, 25th) is a B Corp-certified,
   employed-not-gig annotation vendor with the most enterprise-
   diversified named client list in this category (Google, Microsoft,
   Nvidia, GM, Ford, Walmart). A TIME investigation found its Kenyan
   workers, paid under $2/hour, were tasked with labeling graphic
   descriptions of abuse and violence to build ChatGPT's safety
   filters, reported lasting trauma, and Sama itself canceled the
   OpenAI contract eight months early over the work's severity. So
   what: every prior weakness this file records against Category 1 is
   about data quality or the vendor's own risk exposure (Handshake's
   pay dispute, Mercor's breach). This is different in kind. Minor's
   pipeline has no solicited-labeling step at all, so it has no
   equivalent exposure to name, not because it solved the problem
   Sama's case illustrates but because the architecture never creates
   it. Ledger proposal below.
3. **iMerit closes the lead list with no new argument.** New
   landscape.md entry (Category 1, 26th), the thinnest of the three:
   a regulated-domain niche (healthcare imaging, autonomous systems)
   with no funding event, no named customer, and no independent
   traction number found in any source checked. Recorded for
   completeness; the 18th run's original six-name lead list (Appen,
   Labelbox, Snorkel AI, Sama, iMerit, Defined.ai) is now fully closed.
