## Findings

1. **The reward-model-unreliability claim now has three independent
   sources instead of one.** A memorization study (Verhoeven, Mishra,
   and Shutova, arXiv 2607.24484, revised 2026-10-07) finds
   discriminatively-trained reward models memorize dataset artifacts —
   which model produced a response, response length — rather than a
   generalizable preference signal. A separate NeurIPS 2025 study,
   "One Token to Fool LLM-as-a-Judge" (arXiv 2507.08794), found that
   even the more expensive generative-reward-model architecture can be
   made to award a false positive using nothing but a stray
   punctuation mark, across GPT-o1 and Claude-4 among others. Together
   with the 2026-10-05 brief's WritingPreferenceBench finding (52.7%
   accuracy, barely above chance), that's a pattern across three
   studies rather than one data point. So what: CLAUDE.md §1's
   verifier-gap claim can now open with independent citations instead
   of an assertion Minor would have to source after the fact. Ledger
   proposal below turns this into a drafting instruction for KR4.1's
   lab brief.

2. **Claude's memory feature is no longer one-directional, but it
   isn't symmetric either — it's a second manual copy-paste path.**
   The 2026-10-05 brief's sharpest Category 3 claim was that Claude's
   memory import "does not export anything a competitor could read
   back." Anthropic's own support page, dated by the page itself to
   2026-09-02 and found this run, now documents an export path too:
   ask Claude to write its memory out as text, copy it, paste it
   elsewhere. No file format, no API, nothing claiming a rival platform
   could parse it automatically. So what: "one-directional" should stop
   being said about Claude specifically, but "symmetric" — the
   automated, structure-preserving round trip Major's own claim rests
   on — still hasn't been demonstrated by anyone in this landscape,
   Major included, and the margin for claiming it narrows every time a
   competitor ships a manual workaround that looks like portability.
   Ledger proposal below asks whoever owns the claim to define it as a
   test with a pass/fail result, not a word.

3. **Mercor's first acquisition moves the category's fastest-growing
   vendor one step closer to the verifier layer.** Mercor bought
   Deeptune (announced 2026-07-09, caught by this seat this run), an
   a16z-backed builder of reinforcement-learning "environments" —
   simulated Excel, Salesforce, Slack instances agents practice tasks
   inside. Mercor's CEO framed the deal around the training bottleneck
   moving from raw labeling to the environments models are measured in.
   So what: this is still a constructed, rubric-graded simulation, not
   a real deliverable joined backward to its generation history, so it
   doesn't close CLAUDE.md §1's gap today. But it's the closest any
   named competitor has come to building in the verifier's shape
   rather than the grader's, and it's worth a standing watch item
   rather than an assumption that the gap stays open by default.

4. **The circulating Scale AI S-1 claim is still unconfirmed, three
   weeks after the first EDGAR check.** Re-ran the direct EDGAR search
   flagged as a follow-up in the 2026-09-30 brief. Same result: no
   company named "Scale AI," no S-1 on file. The third-party sites
   repeating the claim still disagree with each other and admit their
   own data may be stale. Housekeeping, not a new finding, but worth
   closing the loop on a flagged open question rather than letting it
   drift.

## The gap

Unchanged in shape from last week: no competitor ties its signal back
to whether real finished work used the generation. What sharpened this
week is the evidence behind why the standard alternative doesn't work —
three independent studies now agree that both the cheap and the
expensive version of reward-model grading fail, for different but
related reasons (memorizing artifacts, or being gamed by content-free
inputs). The outcome record's answer to both failure modes is the same:
it never asks a model to predict or judge quality, it reports what
happened to text after the fact.

A second, narrower gap showed up in the demand-signals ceremony this
run: searches for practitioner complaints about RLHF vendors or memory
portability on Hacker News and Reddit came back thin — mostly vendor
blogs and SEO content, not forum threads. That's worth naming rather
than papering over. It may mean the complaint lives in venues this
seat's searches aren't reaching, or it may mean working AI engineers
aren't yet voicing this specific complaint in public forums the way
researchers are in papers. Worth a different search approach next run
rather than concluding either way from one week's thin result.
