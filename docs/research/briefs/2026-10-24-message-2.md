### 41. A published bound says the gap this repository calls a matcher recall problem has a floor that no matcher work can cross, and one documented assumption in the code turns that floor into a contaminated control arm (high confidence, full text plus four first-hand reads)

**The paper.** *What Lies Beyond Perception: A Partition Theory of the
Limits of AI Evaluation and Oversight*, Ahmed M. Mahran, submitted
2026-10-22, 36 pages, filed in cs.AI, cs.CY and cs.LG [E94]. Read at
full text for its section list, its theorem statements and its own
definition of what it covers, after the abstract page returned a
paraphrase rather than the abstract.

The construction is an evaluator and an examinee, each with a perception
map, and the result is asymmetric. The paper's own statement of what the
letters cover is the reason it reaches this company at all, quoted
verbatim [E94]:

> A letter names an agent rather than a place in a protocol: in the
> statements, A is the evaluator and B the examinee, whether the setting
> is the echo test, the interpretability probe, or the language model
> judge of a scalable-oversight pipeline.

Theorem 9 is stated as "If both conflated stimuli are probed, B fails at
one of them", and Section 4.3 carries the heading "Absence is unprovable
where a probe merges" [E94]. Section 4.6 is the constructive half, and it
treats redundancy across channels that perceive differently, naming the
final answer, the reasoning trace, an interpretability probe and a
system log [E94].

**Where it lands in this repository, read first-hand.** The span
classifier is a lexical matcher with two thresholds. The combined score
is half token containment and half normalized edit similarity,
`THETA_HIGH` is 0.6 and `THETA_LOW` is 0.35, and the file's own opening
comment says the design is deliberately lexical with no embeddings and
no model judging similarity, because every label must be explainable
from those functions and the two thresholds alone [E97]. A span scoring
at or above 0.6 is `survived_mutated`. A span in [0.35, 0.6) is
`no_generation_provenance` with `uncertain` set and the losing candidate
kept on the span. Below 0.35, and when the fuzzy pass surfaced no
candidate at all, `resolve.ts` falls through to a bare
`no_generation_provenance` with no flag and no candidate attached
[E98].

**This organization already knows the gap exists, which is why the
finding is about the framing rather than the gap.** The Limits section
of `skills/adjudicating-uncertain-spans/SKILL.md` names it exactly, and
the sentence is worth quoting because it is accurate and because the
paper sharpens it [E100]:

> A true ancestor that the fuzzy pass never surfaced as a candidate
> appears as `no_generation_provenance` with no candidate at all, and
> nothing in this procedure will find it. That is a recall problem in
> the matcher, not an adjudication problem.

Both halves of that are right. What the paper adds is that the recall of
a single lexical channel is bounded by what that channel can
discriminate, so the problem has a floor rather than a slope. Tuning the
matcher moves spans across the thresholds and does not give the matcher
perception it does not have. On the paper's construction the only thing
that separates two merged stimuli is a channel that merges them
differently, which is Section 4.6's redundancy argument and not a
threshold change. So "recall problem" is true and reads as tunable, and
the sharper statement is that it is a perception bound whose remedy is a
second channel.

**The consequence that makes this concrete rather than philosophical,
and it is a field in the published statistics.** `ursa-major/src/types.ts`
documents `baselineDecayRate` in its own comment as the decay rate over
`no_generation_provenance` spans, described as "text the user wrote
themselves", and explains that this is the repository's background
churn, so agent text decaying at the baseline is not decaying because it
was agent text [E99]. That is a control arm, and it is a good idea. It
also rests on reading the no-provenance class as user-written text,
which is the one thing the merge makes uncertifiable. A generation
reworded past 0.35 lands in the control arm, and the control arm is what
agent text is measured against.

**The direction of the error is the unflattering one twice over.**
`CLAUDE.md` §1 calls `no_generation_provenance` the most valuable
category, because it means the model was never in the running. An
unmatched span is labelled into exactly that category by fallthrough.
And the same span also joins the baseline that agent performance is
compared to. So a matcher miss both inflates the category sold as most
valuable and contaminates the control that would detect the inflation.

**What is missing is a number, and nothing in the record has one.**
`ursa-major/src/stats.ts` contains no occurrence of the word candidate,
so no published statistic distinguishes a `no_generation_provenance`
span that has a losing candidate from one that has none [E99]. The share
is therefore unmeasured rather than small. That is the actionable item
and it is cheap, because this repository already has nine tools named
`measure-*` under `ursa-major/tools/` that do work of exactly this shape
[E99]. Counting spans and characters with no candidate at all, against
those in the uncertain band, on the two existing trials, converts an
unbounded claim into a bounded one. It also tells the engineer whether a
second channel is worth building, which is a decision nobody can make at
present because the quantity it turns on has never been printed.

Confidence is high on every component. The paper was read at full text,
and the four repository reads are first-hand on this branch. What is not
claimed is the size of the effect, which is the thing the measurement
above would produce.

### 42. A benchmark audit measures the curator critique that Ursa Minor's third property asserts, and the paper's own sentence is narrower than the paraphrase of its abstract (high confidence, full text with the sentence verbatim)

**The paper.** *Whose Data, Whose Benchmark? A User-Centric Framework
for Auditing Evaluation Resources*, Mir Tafseer Nayeem and Davood
Rafiei, submitted 2026-10-13, filed in cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CY, cs.ET and
cs.HC [E95]. Read at full text for its section list, its definitions and
its central documentation result.

Its distinction is the useful object, quoted verbatim [E95]:

> Source users, whose content and judgments contribute to an evaluation
> resource, and target users, whose needs and contexts its results are
> intended to inform.

It audits 16 resources, 11 text benchmarks and 5 image-text collections,
and the named text benchmarks are MMLU, GSM8K, HellaSwag, DROP,
WinoGrande, ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, MMLU-Pro, TruthfulQA, MedQA and SVAMP
[E95].

**The result, and the correction that comes with it.** The paraphrase of
the abstract that this seat's instrument returned said that all 16
document source-user context and "none address target-user context"
[E95]. The paper's own sentence is different and narrower, quoted
verbatim [E95]:

> All 16 resources operationalize source-user context through documented
> origins, including examinations, Wikipedia, Reddit, web alt-text, and
> CommonCrawl. Although all mention target-user or evaluation context,
> none operationalize it.

All of them mention the target user. None operationalize it. That is a
claim about documentation practice rather than about awareness, and it
is the better claim, because it says the field knows the distinction
matters and has no machinery for it. A brief resting on the paraphrase
would have reported the field as unaware, which is both wrong and easier
to dismiss by anyone who checked.

**Why it matters to the pitch.** The third of Ursa Minor's three
properties in `CLAUDE.md` §4 is natural task distribution, meaning what
people actually use AI for rather than what a curator picked. That has
been asserted in this repository and never cited to an outside
measurement. This paper is that measurement, on eleven of the
benchmarks a lab buyer would name first, and the shape of its result is
the shape of the assertion. It supplies a second, independent line too,
which is that the resources encode an implicit default user, with
American-centered conventions dominating the matched signals and
geographic visibility in the image-text collections tracking economic
scale [E95].

**Where the claim stops.** The paper is about evaluation resources and
not about reward signal, and it says nothing about outcome records. So
it corroborates the critique Ursa Minor levels at the alternative, and
it is not evidence for Ursa's own artifact. Keeping those apart is the
difference between a citation a researcher respects and one that reads
as recruited.
