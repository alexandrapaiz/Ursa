## Where this period is heading

Three briefs in a row have landed on the same subject from different
categories, and this one makes the shape explicit. The field is moving
from **judging the output** to **recording how the output came to be**,
and the papers arriving now are not arguing for that move. They are
measuring what the first option costs.

The 2026-10-24 hook run found a position paper on Ursa Major's exact
claim in cs.IR. The seventeenth run found an instrumented audit of what
a git repository cannot say about AI provenance in cs.DL. This run found,
in the same category, a formal model of why a bounded check over a
growing artifact loses its evidentiary value, and a 23-version
measurement of why post-hoc detection of model authorship does not
transfer across model generations.

Read together, those four are one argument, and it is Ursa's argument
arriving from outside. **Verification that is sampled decays with
artifact size. Verification that is inferred after the fact decays with
model turnover. Verification that is captured at generation time decays
with neither.** Ursa's outcome record is the third kind. Nothing this
seat has read says so in Ursa's words, and the two papers below now say
the first two halves of it with numbers attached.

The thing a builder should do differently this week follows from the
first half rather than the second, and it is uncomfortable, because the
first half lands on Ursa's own record and not on a competitor's. A
record that grows while the verdict attached to it stays one bit is
exactly the object the bounded-verification paper models, and finding 56
is that object in our own source tree.

## What is RISING

### 1. Bounded verification as a measured quantity, not a worry (high confidence, full text)

`arXiv:2610.05547`, *More Claims, Less Evidence: Bounded Verification of
AI-Generated Digital Knowledge Artifacts*, Bańka and Chudziak, submitted
4 October 2026, cs.AI primary with cs.DL [E165].

The paper gives a Bayesian model of what a partial check implies about a
whole generated artifact, centred on a quantity it names the Predictive
Value of Pass. Read verbatim from the full text:

```
PVP(n,f;b) = P(θ=G|pass) = p / [p + (1-p)q(n,f;b)]
b_min(n,f,η) = min{b ∈ {1,…,n} : PVP(n,f;b) ≥ η}
```

Here `n` is the artifact's claim count, `f` the number of false claims,
`b` the audit budget and `q` the bad-pass probability. The results that
matter to us are three, and the third is the one nobody expects.

1. Evidentiary value falls as artifacts grow under a fixed verification
   budget.
2. The budget needed to hold a target posterior is close to a constant
   **fraction** rather than a constant count. At prior 0.5 with two
   false claims, holding PVP at 0.8 needs 5 of 10 claims checked and 40
   of 80. For 0.9 it is 7 of 10 and 53 of 80.
3. **Adding supported claims around a fixed number of false ones makes
   passing more likely while making a pass less informative.** Growing
   the artifact improves the odds of a clean sample and degrades what a
   clean sample means, at the same time.

Validated on FEVEROUS with FEVER as a robustness check, at artifact
sizes 10, 20, 40 and 80, budgets 3, 5 and 10, false-claim counts 1, 2
and 5, prior 0.5, 1,000 objects per configuration with 20 audit
simulations each under fixed seeds.

**Why it is load-bearing here and not merely apt.** Result 2 is the same
arithmetic this seat used against `MAX_TOKEN_DF` in finding 52, which
the engineer has now shipped the reporting half of. An absolute budget
against a growing object is the defect, and a fraction is the fix shape.
That argument now has a published form, a name and a formula, which is
worth more to us than agreement, because the next time this seat finds
one of these constants it can cite a bound instead of re-deriving one.
Result 3 is new to this record and it is the reason finding 56 is filed
against the acceptance declaration rather than against the span labels.

### 2. Post-hoc detection of model authorship does not survive model turnover (high confidence on the numbers, abstract depth)

`arXiv:2610.11599`, *Large Language Model Turnover Undermines Screening
for Artificial Intelligence-Assisted Scientific Writing*, Nakajima and
Mizuno, submitted 8 October 2026, cs.CL primary with cs.AI, cs.CY, cs.DL
and cs.SI [E166].

Four thousand pre-ChatGPT PNAS abstracts, paired with rewrites by 23 LLM
versions from three vendors released between June 2023 and August 2026.
Detectors were trained under maintenance scenarios from "retrained on
every new version" down to "trained once, never updated". The numbers,
from the abstract:

- Calibrated to flag 1 per cent of human-written abstracts, a detector
  trained on a vendor's past versions catches **above 99 per cent** of
  rewrites just before the sharpest generation boundary and **3.8 per
  cent** just after it.
- Screens covering all 23 versions either flagged **one in eight**
  human-written abstracts or missed **one in three** rewrites of the
  newest version.
- A commercial detector missed most rewrites of the version just after
  the sharpest boundary while flagging almost no human-written abstracts.

Read depth is the abstract page, so this is cited for what the paper is
about and for the figures it states itself, and not for its construction.
An absence claim about its method is not available at this depth.

**Why this is the strongest external support Ursa Minor has yet had, and
it arrives sideways.** Ursa's `no_generation_provenance` class claims
that a span traces to no generation at all. There are exactly two ways to
establish that about a finished artifact. You capture provenance while
the work happens, which is what `ursa run` does, or you infer it
afterwards from the text, which is what a detector does. This paper
measures the second route across three vendors and three years and finds
that its accuracy is not a property of the detector but of which model
generation it last saw. A recall that moves from 99 per cent to 3.8 per
cent across one version boundary is not a weaker version of capture. It
is a different kind of instrument, and it cannot be fixed by a better
detector, because the thing moving is the population.

So the sentence Ursa has been making on principle has a measurement
behind it now: capture-time provenance is stable under model turnover and
detection is not. That belongs in the labs pitch and in the market seat's
positioning, and neither of them has it.

## Finding 56: one sentence from the owner is written onto every record in the project, and each record then carries a declaration that names a step and a quote as though it were about that record alone (high confidence, first-hand, two absence instruments)

**The mechanism, in three files on the engineer's branch** [E167].

`ursa-major/src/bridge/declare.ts:34-43` builds the declaration from one
chat reading:

```ts
const where = `read from chat at step ${verdict.step}: "${verdict.quote}"`
return {
  accepted: verdict.accepted,
  basis: verdict.accepted
    ? `owner-stated satisfied, ${where} (stated tier; the user said it, the system did not infer it)`
    : `owner-stated unsatisfied, ${where}: survived text is not endorsed text, it is not-yet-fixed`,
}
```

`applyVerdict`, in the same file at lines 86 to 106, then writes that one
declaration onto every record the project holds, and says why in its own
doc comment:

```
 * The verdict is a property of the session, and a session's work is
 * spread across the records that session produced, so the reading
 * applies to all of them rather than to a guessed subset.
```

```ts
for (const id of listRecordIds(projectPath)) {
  const record = loadRecord(projectPath, id)
  if (!record) continue
  record.signals = declaredSignals(record, declaration, verdict, note)
  declared.push(id)
```

The reasoning is sound and this finding does not dispute it. A verdict
read from a session genuinely is a property of the session. The defect is
what the record says afterwards.

**What each record then carries.** `src/types.ts:926-934` gives the
episode three acceptance fields and no fourth:

```ts
episode: {
  steps: number
  generations: number
  /** null = undeclared: no owner declaration was given; never inferred */
  accepted: boolean | null
  /** terminal acceptance is often tacit — retention without complaint */
  acceptanceStatedInChat: boolean
  acceptanceBasis: string
}
```

And `src/viewer.ts:620-628` renders it to whoever opens the record:

```
'Episode: ' + fmt(ep.steps) + ' steps · ' + fmt(ep.generations) +
' generations · ' + verdict + ' · basis: ' + ep.acceptanceBasis
```

So a reader of one record sees `ACCEPTED` and a basis string naming step
41 and a verbatim quote. Nothing on the record says that thirty-nine
other records carry the identical step, the identical quote and the
identical verdict from the same sentence.

**This is not merely silence about breadth. The basis string is
positively misleading about it.** A field that said only `accepted: true`
would be under-informative. A field that names one step and one quote
asserts specificity that the write path does not have, and it asserts it
in the one place a reader would look to check. The form is per-record and
the fact is per-session.

**The breadth is computed and then dropped at exactly the wrong
boundary.** `src/bridge/index.ts:58` carries it:

```ts
/** ids of the records now carrying this verdict as their declaration */
declaredRecords: string[]
```

That field is on `OverlayPayload`, which is the encrypted blob the user's
own overlay page decrypts, set at `src/bridge/index.ts:249` from
`applied.declared`. So the number exists, it is correct, and it is
delivered to **the transparency surface**. It is absent from the outcome
record, which is **the commercial surface**: the thing `CLAUDE.md` §1
defines as the artifact, the thing `ursa publish` can put at a public
address, and the thing the `clearing-a-record-for-release` skill governs
quoting into a design note, a ledger entry or a pull request.

Ursa has built the honest disclosure and pointed it at the party who
needs it least. The owner already knows she said it once.

**Why the paper is what makes this a defect rather than a note.** Result
3 of [E165] is the part that bites. Growing the artifact makes a clean
pass more likely and makes the pass less informative, simultaneously. A
project accumulating records is an artifact growing while the budget
behind its verdict stays at one owner sentence, so every record added to
the project weakens the declaration on all of them, and nothing in the
data says the denominator moved. Result 2 gives the fix shape: what has
to be recorded is not a better verdict but the **budget** the verdict
rested on, because PVP is not computable from a pass alone.

**The direction of the error is the bad one, and it is the same direction
as finding 52's.** A buyer or an adjudicator reading forty records each
saying `ACCEPTED` with a specific quote will read forty declarations.
There was one. The over-reading inflates the strength of the label on
every record the owner did not individually consider, and `CLAUDE.md` §0
is explicit that acceptance is never inferred and only the owner's
declaration counts. The write path honours that rule for the session and
breaks its spirit for the record, because a declaration stamped on a
record the owner never opened is closer to an inference than the rule
intends.

**Absence instruments, both run** [E168]. A grep over tracked files for
`declaredRecords`, `recordsCovered`, `declarationBreadth`, `coveredRecords`,
`appliesTo` and `fan-out` returns five hits, all of them the overlay
payload field, its two tests and the one design note that introduced it
(`docs/design/verdict-to-record.md:130`, where it is annotated `// NEW`).
A pull-request search in all states for `acceptanceBasis` and
`applyVerdict` returns one pull request, #24 from 2026-09-26, which is
the branch that built this path. Neither instrument finds the breadth on
the record or anyone proposing to put it there.

**Who can fix it, and the smallest version.** The engineer. One field on
`episode`, set from the length of the list `applyVerdict` already builds,
plus one clause in the basis string when that length exceeds one. The
viewer line is already a concatenation and takes the clause without
restructuring. This is not this seat's call to make and it is filed, not
decided.

**What it is not.** The current `minor-aggregate` disclosure does not
carry acceptance at all. `projectForMinor` in `src/disclosure.ts:234` is
counters per domain, model and window [E170]. So no lab holds a weakened
acceptance label today and this is not a live leak. It is a defect in the
artifact, and the artifact is the product.

## Finding 57: the second absolute constant in the same loop is not finding 52's twin, and the measurement says so (refuted hypothesis, reported as a negative result)

**The hypothesis, which was this run's own and looked strong.**
`ursa-major/src/match.ts` holds two absolute constants read by the same
block of the fuzzy pass:

```
match.ts:12  export const MAX_TOKEN_DF = 500
match.ts:14  export const FUZZY_TOP_K = 25
```

`resolve.ts:411` scores only `candidates.slice(0, FUZZY_TOP_K)`. Finding
52's argument applies word for word: a fixed count of candidates scored
out of a candidate set that grows with the corpus, which is [E165]'s
result 1 exactly. And the failure would be worse than finding 52's,
because a span whose true source is cut by the top-K slice gets
`noCandidate: 'scored_below_floor'` from the field the engineer shipped
today, and that value's own comment reads "The matcher looked."
`FUZZY_TOP_K` appears in two source files and in no document, skill or
prior brief, and in no pull request in any state [E168], so nothing had
been written about it either.

**The measurement, and it refuted the hypothesis** [E169]. The rig
imports the shipped `segment`, `normalize`, `tokens`, `containment`,
`combinedScore` and `levSimilarity` and the four shipped constants, so
the predicate under test is the product's own rather than a restatement
of it. It builds a proxy generation corpus from this repository's 164
markdown files under `docs/`, 79,713 prose segments, shuffled under a
fixed seed before prefixes are taken. For each probe it takes a real
segment, mutates it, and asks where the true source ranks by containment
among the candidates the prefilter actually seeds, in `resolve()`'s own
insertion order.

| generation segments | probes | true source cut by the top-K slice | would-be `survived_mutated` lost to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 400 | 0 (0.00 per cent) | 0 |
| 5,000 | 321 | 0 (0.00 per cent) | 0 |
| 10,000 | 304 | 0 (0.00 per cent) | 0 |
| 25,000 | 288 | 0 (0.00 per cent) | 0 |
| 50,000 | 302 | 0 (0.00 per cent) | 0 |
| 79,713 | 314 | 1 (0.32 per cent) | 0 |

Run twice, with a light mutation of one interior word and with a heavy
one replacing a word in four, and the two tables are identical. Run again
restricted to short spans of four to eight distinct tokens, where
containment ties are coarsest and the hypothesis should have been
strongest, and the single cut case appears at 0.98 per cent of 102 probes
with still nothing lost.

**Why it does not bite, which is the part worth keeping.** Containment is
a strong enough ranking key that the true source stays inside the first
25 at every size measured. The one case where it was cut was a span whose
true source would not have cleared `THETA_HIGH` anyway, so no label
changed. The two constants are the same arithmetic and they are not the
same risk, because `MAX_TOKEN_DF` gates whether a span is **seen at all**
while `FUZZY_TOP_K` only truncates a list that is already sorted by a key
correlated with the answer.

**The bound, the same one finding 52 carried.** The corpus is a proxy.
It is this repository's prose rather than conversation transcripts, and
prose written by many runs about one subject repeats its vocabulary more
than a general corpus does, which if anything makes containment ties
**more** common here than in the real population. So the negative result
transfers in the direction that matters. A corpus whose segments are far
more alike than real ones still did not push a true source past 25.

**No defect is filed and nothing is asked of the engineer.** The reason
this is in the brief at all is that the hypothesis is the obvious next
one after finding 52, it is cheap to form and expensive to test, and a
future run that forms it can now read this table instead of rebuilding
the rig.

## Top extraction targets for the skill agent

**One target, and it is first because the evidence is a formula rather
than a technique.**

### Target 1 — Sizing a check against the thing being checked

**What it is.** Procedure and judgement for the question "I cannot look at
all of it, so what does looking at some of it buy me?" The steps are
naming the artifact's unit count, naming the budget actually spent,
reading the pass as a posterior rather than a verdict, and refusing to
let the artifact grow without the budget growing with it. The tradeoff is
that a fraction-based budget costs linearly in artifact size and a
constant one is free, and the failure mode is the one [E165] names: a
bigger artifact makes a clean sample more likely and less meaningful at
the same time, so the number that looks reassuring is the one to distrust.

**Evidence, and why it is enough for a skill rather than a note.** The
paper supplies the formula, the budget rule and the counter-intuitive
third result [E165], read in full. This repository supplies three
independent instances that the procedure would have caught, all
first-hand and all in `ursa-major/src/`: `MAX_TOKEN_DF` at 500 (finding
52, now reported on by the engineer), the one-sentence declaration
fanned onto every record (finding 56 above), and one case where the
procedure correctly says **do nothing**, which is `FUZZY_TOP_K` (finding
57). That last one is what makes it a skill and not a slogan. A procedure
that flags every absolute constant is noise. This one has a worked
example of declining to flag, with the reason.

**Where it would apply, named so the skill agent can check the fit
before drafting.** `skills/adding-a-bound-to-the-record-gate` and
`skills/clearing-a-record-for-release` both govern checks whose budget is
currently implicit, and `skills/quoting-a-number-from-an-outcome-record`
governs reporting a number whose denominator is the question this skill
asks. The skill agent is free to reject the ranking and should read those
three before drafting, because the honest outcome may be a clause in one
of them rather than a fourth file.

**No second target.** The detection paper [E166] is read at abstract
depth, which licenses positioning and not procedure, and a skill drawn
from an abstract would be exactly the error the charter's read-depth rule
was written against. The cs.DL judgement cluster is listing depth. Zero
is better than padded.

**One proposal, and it is the minimum the charter's own text requires.**

`prompts/research-agent.md` moves `cs.DL` out of "Qualifying under the
rule above and never opened" and into "Opened as a listing at least

findings, both of which came out of that listing on first contact
[E164], [E165], [E166]. The diff is two lines and changes no rule.

That is a proposal. The owner's merge is the promotion.

**What this seat is watching and not yet acting on.**

- **Negative results have no place in this charter and now have one
  instance.** Finding 57 is the first refuted hypothesis this seat has
  written up as such, and a grep for "refuted", "did not hold" and
  "hypothesis" across every brief in `docs/research/briefs/` returns
  nothing of that shape, though two briefs do report negative results
  about measurement methods. One instance is an anecdote and the charter
  says a proposal needs a pattern, so no rule is proposed. What is
  recorded is the cost if the pattern forms: a dead hypothesis that
  leaves no trace is re-formed by the next run at full price, and the
  cheapest hypotheses to form are the ones adjacent to a finding that
  just landed, which is exactly where the next run will be looking.

  rather than a list.** Ten categories opened, two qualifying and not,
  and two of them need slicing because they exceed 5,000 entries a month.
  A run has time for one or two complete listings. The set says which
  categories qualify and says nothing about which to spend a run on, so
  the choice is made fresh under time pressure every run, which is the
  failure mode the set was written to close one level down. Not proposed
  because the right rule is not yet obvious from the evidence, and a
  guessed one would be worse than the judgement it replaced.
