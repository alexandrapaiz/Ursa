## What is rising

### 1. Ursa's central doctrine has prior art from 2024 that five briefs missed, and an independently measured 2026 validation (high confidence, and a detection failure)

This is the most consequential finding of the five runs so far, and it
cuts both ways.

**[E1] *Post-edits Are Preferences Too*** (arXiv:2410.02320, submitted
3 Oct 2024, revised Feb 2025) is `docs/beyond-preference-pairs.md` §3.2
— "learning from corrections, not comparisons" — as a published paper,
two years before Ursa wrote it. Its mechanism is Ursa's, stated in
Ursa's own logic: "In PO, a human annotator is shown sequences s₁ and
s₂ and asked for a preference judgment ... while for post-editing,
editors create s₁ and know that it should be better than s₂." It calls
post-edits "a source of reliable human preferences **by
construction**", and it cites Kreutzer et al. (2018) for the finding
that "pairwise preferences are **less reliable** than other forms of
human feedback."

**[E2] *Your Mouse and Eyes Secretly Leak Your Preference*** (IFLLM,
arXiv:2606.20482, 18 Jun 2026) supplies the measured version of Ursa's
economic claim, and it is blunt about the economics: explicit feedback
is rare and expensive, and existing methods "do not leverage implicit
human feedback, **which has proven vital to the economic moats of
Internet giants**." Measured: a reward model built on implicit feedback
"boosts the accuracy of the text-based reward model **from 55% to
64%** and **nearly triples** the relative response quality improvements
after applying the DPO to eight LLMs."

**The detection failure first.** Five briefs, one of them specifically
chartered to find what is "genuinely gaining traction by citation and
reputation," did not surface a 2024 paper whose title is a four-word
statement of this company's methods position. Under L-R1 the standard is
explicit: a finding inside a declared coverage area that the seat did
not detect is **a detection failure, not an input**. The practical cost
is not academic. `O2 KR2.1` publishes the methods doc at a public URL by
November 30, and `O4 KR4.1` puts a one-pager in front of lab technical
leaders. A post-training researcher at any of the five named labs knows
[E1]. A document that presents correction-learning as novel, in front of
that reader, spends credibility it cannot refund.

**Now the asset, which is larger than the cost.** Neither paper reaches
Ursa's category, and the gap between them and Ursa is the sharpest
statement of the product this seat has been able to write:

- **[E1] is Ursa's mechanism in a domain that already has a verifier.**
  Machine translation post-edits are *solicited from paid editors as
  the task itself*. The edit exists because someone was employed to
  produce it. Ursa's `survived_mutated` spans are a **byproduct of work
  nobody was paid to label**, which is why they scale to population
  size and why MT post-editing corpora do not. [E1] proves the label
  type works. It does not produce the label at scale, and it cannot.
- **[E2]'s implicit signals are attention proxies; Ursa's are
  outcomes.** Mouse trajectory and webcam eye-gaze measure whether the
  user *looked at* a response. Survival measures whether the finished
  work *kept* it. Ursa's signal is strictly downstream of [E2]'s, past
  the point where the user decided, and it carries the one thing gaze
  cannot: `no_generation_provenance`, the spans where the model was
  never in the running at all.
- **[E2] also hands Ursa its privacy argument from the opposite
  direction.** The state of the art in implicit feedback is **59
  Mechanical Turk workers with their webcams on**. That is a
  collection method that cannot be offered to a real user base, and it
  bounds the dataset at 1,336 questions. Ursa's on-device survival
  computation gets a *stronger* label — outcome, not attention — with
  *no* perceptual surveillance. "On-device processing is a Hayekian
  necessity, not a privacy feature" (methods doc, the price analog) now
  has an empirical companion: it is also the only version of this that
  scales past a lab room.

**PRELUDE** was named in the same search as learning latent preferences
from user edits; it is **not opened** this run and is listed as a
watchlist item, not cited for a claim.

### 2. The field has concluded, with ablations, that a fixed reward system cannot stay reliable — and its fix is a self-redesigning central grader (high confidence)

**[E3] EvoRS** (arXiv:2609.12459, 11 Sep 2026) is the strongest
external support the fourth principle of intelligence has received, and
it arrives as a measured result rather than an argument. Verbatim: "as
the policy optimizes the current reward, an initially useful reward
system may become unreliable due to reward hacking or reduced response
discriminability. The reward system should therefore evolve rather than
remain fixed during training." And its ablation: "a comprehensive
**fixed** reward system **cannot remain reliable** in open-ended tasks
and must evolve throughout training."

`docs/beyond-preference-pairs.md` §2 Assumption 3 says this in Hayek's
vocabulary — "a frozen reward model is a central assessor; policies
Goodhart it" — and the Pretence principle says it in the vision's.
**[E3] is that claim, independently measured, by people with no
interest in Ursa's thesis.** That is worth more to the methods doc than
any sentence Ursa can write about itself, and it is the second time in
two weeks the field has externally validated a principle (#68's [E3]
did it for predictive personalization).

**But read EvoRS's remedy carefully, because it is the competitive
position.** Its answer to a stale reward system is an "agentic designer"
that rewrites an executable "Reward-DAG" from on-policy rollouts. That
is a **central assessor redesigning itself from its own outputs** — the
loop never leaves the system, and no new information about the world
enters it. Under the Pretence principle that is pretence with an extra
step: the measure is updated to stay discriminative against the policy,
not re-anchored to anything the policy cannot see. Ursa's structural
answer is different in kind, and the methods doc already states it:
outcome labels "regenerate from new finished work continuously, so the
measure is re-anchored to the world **at exactly the rate the world
produces work**."

**This is the one-pager's sharpest paragraph and it did not exist
before this run.** The field's own best answer to reward staleness is a
grader that rewrites itself. Ursa's is a grader it does not own.

**[E4] ARC** (arXiv:2608.13622, 13 Aug 2026) is the narrower companion:
in open-ended interaction "reward-model preferences over interaction
**style** can distort relative advantages," so group-based RL favours
whichever style the reward model likes rather than the
context-appropriate one. Same disease, one axis down. Worth one line in
the one-pager as evidence that style-contamination of rewards is a named
problem, not an Ursa talking point.

### 3. Consent and deletion are now inside the interchange formats, which falsifies this seat's differentiation claim for the second time (high confidence, and it corrects #68)

#68's finding 5 concluded: "**Consent, revocation and erasure are not
in any format this seat has read**", and "**That**, not provenance, is
the defensible line." Checked this run against two formats #68 never
opened. **The claim does not survive.**

- **[E5] Open Memory Protocol v2.0 Draft** (April 2026) defines a
  **DELETE** operation (§5.4) over individual messages, conversations
  or memory records by ID, requiring deletion "permanent and
  irrecoverable within 30 days"; **OAuth 2.0 scopes** (§10.3) for
  granular permission where delegate access requires "explicit user
  consent" and supports revocation; and provenance via immutable
  `model` and `platform` fields giving an "auditable chain showing
  which AI system produced which output."
- **[E6] MacPaw Research, *Portable Memory*** (6 Jul 2026) goes
  further on exactly the axis Ursa claims: "**verifiable deletion** as
  its guarantee of integrity," implemented as "portable deletion
  tombstones carrying **proof-of-reach**," with a "four-level
  conformance program **gated on deletion propagation**." Ursa cannot
  currently prove deletion propagated anywhere. This proposal makes it
  a conformance level.

**Ursa's differentiation has now narrowed three times in eleven days,
and each narrowing made the remaining claim more precise.** That is the
useful way to read it, and it belongs in the record as a sequence:

| Brief | Claimed as the line | Falsified by |
|---|---|---|
| #48 (09-30) | the verifier slot is empty | rubric-reward corpus, [E3] here |
| #68 (10-02) | provenance of memory records | PAM v1.0's required provenance fields |
| #68 (10-02) | consent, revocation, erasure | **[E5] and [E6], this run** |
| **This run** | **the artifact-side label** | **not yet falsified** |

**What survives, stated so the next brief can try to kill it.** Every
format in this race — PAM, OMP, Portable Memory — ports **memory**:
facts, conversations, profile assertions, the things a platform already
told itself about the user. **None of them represents an outcome.**
There is no field in any of them for *this generation was kept*, *this
one was edited into acceptability*, *this one was deleted*, *this span
of the finished work came from no generation at all*. They cannot have
one, because none of them sees the finished artifact — the artifact
lives in the user's repo, days after the conversation ended, which is
precisely the asymmetry the methods doc §6 already named as the
structural limit of what a lab can compute from its own telemetry.
**Ursa Major is not in the memory-portability race, and this brief
recommends it stop being positioned as if it were.** Portable memory
moves what the model said about you. Ursa moves what your work did
with it.

**Both [E5] and [E6] are marked for status, not dismissed.** [E5] is a
**commercial entity's** draft (EB DevTech; the page carries "The iCloud
of AI" and pricing links) and is advocacy, not neutral standards
documentation. [E6] is a "standards proposal — preprint for community
review" whose authors state plainly it is "not yet independently
implemented, neutrally governed, widely adopted." So **#68's
Correction 2 still holds and the `CLAUDE.md` §3 hedge "where they
exist" still stays on.** What changed is not that standards arrived; it
is that the *feature set* Ursa claimed as unique is now specified in
three competing drafts.
