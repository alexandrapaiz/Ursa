## Corrections to this seat own prior brief

**The last brief overstated what arXiv:2610.04710 does, and the full
text turns out to be more useful in a different place.** Its finding 3
reported, at abstract depth and at medium-high confidence, that the
paper "formalizes the construction Ursa engineer shipped on the same
day, including a first-class home for `no_generation_provenance`." This
run read the full text [E1]. Three things are now clear.

The paper is a verification framework, not a reward-signal framework.
Its subject is a two-tier logic over agent trajectories, its unit of
output is an assurance ledger in section 3.1, and its four categories
are kinds of evidence contract rather than kinds of span. Quoted
verbatim, they are an **exact contract**, which "establishes phi on
every run of Sigma under stated assumptions", a **suite contract**,
which "requires N(App_r) >= k and N(not phi) = 0 on a named suite T", a
**risk contract**, which "requires Pr(not phi) <= epsilon with
confidence 1 minus delta for a named distribution", and a **descriptive
claim**, which is "a statistic that cannot discharge exact or risk
contracts" [E1].

Its residual is therefore not the Ursa residual. Section 6 is titled
"The residual and how to reduce it", and the residual is the part of a
verification claim that the assembled methods leave unestablished. The
Ursa `no_generation_provenance` is a span of a finished artifact that
descends from no generation at all. Both schemes name their leftover and
treat it as the interesting part, and that is the whole of the
resemblance. The last brief phrasing would tell the engineer that
published prior art formalizes the construction being built here, and it
does not.

And the paper touches no human edits. It evaluates on 456 released
tau-squared-bench telecom trajectories, being 114 tasks at four trials
each, with no new experimental runs executed, and the only human
involvement is the extraction and audit of 20 policy rules, two of which
were repaired [E1]. So it is not evidence about what happens when real
people edit real work, which is the one thing the Ursa artifact is made
of.

**What it is good for instead, and this is the part worth keeping.** The
four contracts are a ready vocabulary for the question a lab will ask
first about an outcome record, which is what each line of it is entitled
to claim. Ursa already has both halves of that distinction in the
repository and does not mark which is which. The 48 violation fixtures
under `ursa-major/fixtures/violations/` are a suite contract in the
exact sense of the paper, being a named suite with a requirement of zero
counterexamples [E7]. The span percentages under `stats.byClass` are
descriptive claims in its exact sense, being statistics over one corpus
that establish nothing about the next record [E6]. A reader of a record
today cannot tell the two apart, and the paper gives a four-word
vocabulary and a ledger shape for telling them apart. That is a real
borrow, and it is cheaper than anything the last brief proposed from the
same paper.

## What is rising

### 1. Override-as-preference-signal has a formal literature, it is five and a half months old, and it names a bias the Ursa record has no term for (high confidence, full text read)

**arXiv:2604.28010, submitted 2026-04-30 and revised 2026-05-15, seven
authors from an operating clinical deployment** [E2]. Its opening move
is the one `CLAUDE.md` section 1 makes: "We reframe clinician overrides
of clinical AI recommendations as implicit preference data, the same
signal structure exploited by reinforcement learning from human
feedback, but richer: the annotator is a domain expert, the alternatives
carry real consequences, and downstream outcomes are observable."

That is the Ursa Minor claim, in another domain, published five and a
half months ago. This seat has searched prior art on the outcome record
every run and has never opened it. The shape of the miss is the same one
the 2026-10-09 brief filed against itself for the Major side, where an
eighteen-author position paper on the exact Major claim sat five months
old and was found in one search. So this is the second instance of one
pattern, and the pattern is that this seat searches its own vocabulary
rather than the claim. Ursa says `survived_mutated` and says the
mutation is the correction. The literature says override, deviation, and
disagreement. Nothing in eleven runs of keyword search crossed that gap.

**The part that is a genuine threat rather than a parallel.** Table 2 of
the paper gives a five-category override taxonomy, quoted verbatim:
**Type I (Context)**, "Clinician has private patient information not in
s_t"; **Type II (Judgment)**, "Same information as system; genuine
clinical disagreement"; **Type III (Workflow)**, "Agreement in
principle; different action due to time, staffing, or tool
constraints"; **Type IV (Protocol)**, "Institutional protocol or
standing order supersedes recommendation"; and **Type V (Capability)**,
"Clinician lacks domain-specific skill or confidence to execute" [E2].

Only Type II is the thing the Ursa record assumes it is measuring. Under
the other four, a correct generation receives a rejecting label. And
section 3.5 names the consequence, quoted verbatim, as **suppression
bias**, meaning "the systematic suppression of correct-but-difficult
recommendations in environments where mean clinician capability for the
relevant domain is below the execution threshold" [E2].

Read that against the Ursa artifact. An outcome record labels a span
`generated_deleted` when the work threw it away and `survived_mutated`
when the work edited it, and `CLAUDE.md` section 1 says the mutation is
the correction. Neither label distinguishes a generation that was worse
from a generation the user could not use, would have had to learn
something to use, or lacked the surrounding context to evaluate. At the
population scale Ursa Minor sells into, that difference is not noise
that averages out, because capability is correlated across users. It is
a systematic gradient toward what users can readily absorb and away from
what is better but harder, which is close to the opposite of what a lab
buys this signal to fix. The remedy the paper proposes is a capability
model trained jointly with the reward model, decomposed into an
execution capability and an alignment capability, by alternating
optimization [E2].

**The absence claim, with its instrument.** Nothing in the Ursa record
carries a capability term or names this confound. The instruments were a
grep for "suppression bias", "capability model", "user capability" and
"skill of the user" across `docs/`, `ursa-major/`, `prompts/` and
`CLAUDE.md`, which returned nothing, and a search of pull requests
across all states, open and closed and merged, on "suppression" and on
"capability", whose only hits are a write-capability design in PR #32
and unrelated uses in the OKR check-ins and two other seat titles [E8].
Per the charter, `main` is not the memory of this organization, so the
closed-and-open pull request search is the instrument that makes this
claim sayable.

**And the opportunity, which is the reason to rank this first.** The
paper has no data. Section 1.4 says "Validation of specific framework
predictions is underway and will be reported separately", and the full
text contains no sample sizes, no deployment metrics and no empirical
results at all [E2]. It is a framework that emerged from operational
work and has not yet met it. Ursa has the opposite problem, holding real
outcome records and no theory of the confound. A framework with no data
and a dataset with no framework is the most favourable configuration
this seat has found for Ursa in eleven runs, and it argues for citation
and contact rather than for quiet borrowing.

### 2. The first adaptive-counterparty simulation of consumer AI agents puts a number on how much of the gain survives (medium confidence, abstract page read only)

**arXiv:2610.05823, submitted 2026-10-05, Haonan Huang and Joey Xiao**
[E3]. An agent-based simulation of a rental market, with language models
as consumers, assistants and six adaptive sellers holding algorithmic
pricing tools, and half the population given an assistant under either
an advisory or an executing mandate. Executing assistants cut adopter
spending by 13.7 dollars per renter-day against frozen sellers, and by
8.7 dollars after the sellers adapt, so roughly a third of the gain is
recovered by the other side. Consumers with no assistant were barely
harmed, at plus 0.4 dollars against a behavioral prediction of plus 3.6.

**Why this matters here, and the limit on it.** Ursa Major does not
transact, so this is adjacent to its value proposition rather than about
it. What it is about is the structure `CLAUDE.md` section 5 calls the
central strategic problem, which is a counterparty that can adapt to
your existence. This is the first quantified instance this seat has seen
of the adaptive-counterparty question in the consumer-agent setting, and
its answer is that about a third of the modeled gain is recoverable by
the adapting side while the bystanders are unharmed. Treat it as a frame
for asking the same question on the lab side, which is how much of the
value of the Ursa signal survives labs adapting to its availability, and
not as evidence about Ursa. Read at the abstract page only, so the
mechanism behind the two figures is unverified here and the confidence
is set accordingly.

## What the engineer should know before building

**The four evidence contracts in [E1] are worth borrowing, and the
borrow is small.** `ursa-major/fixtures/violations/` already is a suite
contract and `stats.byClass` already is a descriptive claim, in the

inside the record, rather than leaving a reader to infer it, is the
difference between a lab auditor reading a percentage as a property of
the system and reading it as a statistic over one corpus. This is
additive and it touches no bound.

**And before the next schema change, the capability confound belongs in
the vocabulary of the record rather than in a brief.** Nothing in
finding 1 requires Ursa to build a capability model. It requires the
record to stop asserting, by the shape of its labels, that a mutation is
always a correction. A field that can hold why alongside what happened,
even left mostly unset, is what keeps the question answerable later.

## Meta-review — one proposal

**The proposal is two corrections to one file,
`prompts/research-agent.md`, and both are about how the evidence rules
of this seat are stated. The file edit itself could not be made this
run, because the host volume has no space, so the proposal is stated
here in full and the edit is left for the next run or the owner.**

**The pattern, which is why this is a proposal and not an anecdote.**
The read-depth discipline of this seat is written entirely around
negative claims. The charter says an absence claim about a paper
requires the full text, that an absence claim about the work of this
organization requires a pull-request search, and that a verifying query
must not name the claim it is testing. All three are good and this run
used all three. But the last three briefs have each had to correct a
**positive** claim made at abstract depth. The 2026-10-13 brief
corrected prior work on two instruments, the 2026-10-16 brief corrected
its predecessor, and this brief opens by correcting finding 3 of the
2026-10-16 brief, which asserted at abstract depth that a paper
formalizes the Ursa construction when its full text shows a different
object evaluated on no human edits [E1]. Three instances in three
consecutive runs, all the same shape, and the charter currently warns
about the other direction only.

**The amendment**, to be added to the negative-claim paragraph in Step
0: a claim about what a paper **does** contain, when it is load-bearing
for a finding or a skill target, carries the read depth that supports it
and takes its confidence from that depth, and an abstract page licenses
a claim about the subject of a paper but not about its construction.

**The second correction** is the stale header premise in record finding
4. The instruction stands and its reason does not, so the reason is
restated as the true one, which is that the newest briefs are routinely
unmerged rather than that `main` holds none of them.

**This is a proposal and not a promotion.** It also carries the
structural cost the 2026-10-16 brief named, which is that a brief
touching `prompts/` is Tier C under the PM classification and therefore
waits on the owner. Since the edit was not made this run, this pull
request does not touch `prompts/` at all, which means the findings and
the skill targets can land without waiting on the charter decision.
