## Corrections to this seat's own prior briefs

This comes first because it changes claims the last two briefs made,
and because the charter says correcting a prior brief is part of the
job rather than an exception to it.

**1. [E3] is a jury-based instrument, and the last brief called its
core mechanical.** The 2026-10-13 brief read [E3] at its abstract page,
reported the four competence axes as "shares of sound research
opportunities taken, which is mechanical", and flagged only the
subjective persona layer as possibly judge-produced, saying nobody may
cite that layer until someone opens the full text. The full text, read
this run, inverts both halves of that reading. Layer **L2 of the
instrument is literally named "juries"**, Section 5.2 is titled "Score
stability across rescoring and alternative judge models", Related Work
has a subsection on LLM judges, and the paper reports that **a run took
a median of 652 judge calls**. So the instrument is judge-dependent
throughout rather than in one optional layer.

What is mechanical is the constraint around the juries, and that
distinction is the useful part. **L1 is "card extraction with quote
verification" and L3 is "deterministic assembly."** Juries produce the
judgements, code checks that each one quotes the record, and assembly
is deterministic. The honest description is a hybrid, where a model
supplies the reading and code refuses a reading that is not grounded in
an excerpt.

And the persona layer turns out to be the least objectionable part
rather than the most. **T2 to T6 are "the share of opportunities in
which the agent chose one pole"**, they are profiled rather than
scored, and the paper says they carry no preferred direction. T1 is the
share of output tokens spent on actions. The last brief's
unquotable flag is lifted, and the caution moves to the whole
instrument instead.

**2. The claim that no human appears anywhere in this territory's

## Where this is heading

One current runs through everything opened this run, and it is sharper
than the repo's current framing of its own advantage.

**The field has converged on the right objects and keeps substituting a
model for the missing human.** Four papers, four research communities,
one shape:

- **[E7]**, the canonical treatment of learning from user edits, has
  the theory for exactly Ursa's `survived_mutated` class, and its
  experiments contain no user edits. Section 5.1 says why, in its own
  words: "Interactively generating coactive feedback from humans would
  be too expensive for our experiments. We thus simulate coactive
  feedback." The simulator is an LLM expert policy, and the reward the
  whole evaluation is scored against is that expert's own DPO-implied
  reward.
- **[E9]** builds a user-sovereign personalization architecture and
  scores it with Gemini 2.5 Flash as judge, then reports in its own
  preliminary study that correlation between the judge and its eight
  human raters was "positive but moderate".
- **[E3]** reads only the execution record, never a reference answer
  and never an outcome score, which is the right object, and spends a
  median of 652 judge calls per run to do it. Its own limitation
  section draws the conclusion: that cost "suits offline evaluation
  rather than a training reward."
- **[E10]** is the exception that shows the pattern is a choice. It
  flags rather than scores, it types every claim by the strength of the
  method that produced it, and it reports what no method accounted for
  as a first-class output.

So the direction of travel is settled and the bottleneck is not. Everyone
now agrees the signal lives in the artifact and the record rather than
in a rating. Nobody has a cheap, non-model source of truth over them,
so each system substitutes a model and then measures the substitution's
error. **Ursa's artifact is the one construction in this set where no
substitution is needed, because the label is the work's own fate rather
than anyone's reading of it.** That is what `CLAUDE.md` §1 means by the
label being supplied by the artifact, and it is now a claim with
external corroboration rather than an assertion.

**What a builder should do differently this week.** Stop arguing the
premise from first principles. Three of this week's primaries supply
the citations that `docs/beyond-preference-pairs.md` has been arguing
without, and one of them supplies a regret bound. The work is not new
machinery. It is attaching the external evidence to the two artifacts a
lab is meant to audit, which are the methods doc and the lab brief.

## What is rising

### 1. Ursa's `survived_mutated` class has a named theory, a regret bound, and an eighteen-month-old literature that has never once run on real human edits (high confidence, full text read)

**[E7] Tucker, Brantley, Cahall and Joachims, *Coactive Learning for
Large Language Models using Implicit User Feedback*, ICML 2024, PMLR
235, pages 48809 to 48822.** The last brief carried this as an
unverified lead with no identifier, cited for nothing, and said nobody
may quote it yet. **The identifier is now resolved and the full text is
read**, so the restriction is lifted.

**What it gives Ursa, and this is the single most useful external
result this seat has found.** The abstract states the premise in terms
Ursa already uses: "users provide implicit feedback whenever they edit
the text y proposed by an LLM", and "coactive learning merely requires
that the edited text ȳ is an improvement over the proposed text y."
That is `survived_mutated` as `CLAUDE.md` §1 defines it, where the
mutation is the correction expressed as an edit rather than a
complaint.

**The formal condition is the part to carry.** Section 3.1 defines
strictly α-informative feedback as

> R*(x,ȳ) − R*(x,y) ≥ α (R*(x,y*) − R*(x,y))

with a slack variant that adds ξ to model noisy preferences, including
preferences that "point into the wrong direction". Section 3.2's
Theorem 3.1 bounds coactive regret by the mean slack over α plus the
pairwise learner's cumulative loss over α. **The load-bearing sentence
for Ursa is that "α ∈ (0,1] is an unknown parameter, but we will see
that knowledge of α is not needed to run the learning algorithm."**
Ursa never has to claim how much better an edit was. It only has to
record that the edit happened and survived, which is exactly what the
resolver produces.

**And the gap in it is Ursa's entire reason to exist.** The theory is
for human edits. The validation has none. Feedback is generated by an
expert policy trained with DPO, the reward function is that same
expert's implied reward, and the edit-based condition is implemented by
"resampling the last n tokens of the policy's response y using the
expert once." Meanwhile the paper's own Impact Statement claims it
"shows that human edit data can be a valuable source of feedback" and
that "passively collected edit data can improve performance in writing
assistance tasks." Both claims are about human data the experiments did
not use. The theory is sound and the premise is untested on people,
because ICML's canonical treatment said collecting the data was too
expensive.

**So the Ursa Minor sentence this supports, which is stronger than
anything in the repo now:**

> The reward signal we produce has had a theory with a regret bound
> since ICML 2024. What it has never had is data, because the field's
> own canonical treatment simulated the edits with a language model
> after judging real collection too expensive. Ursa Major collects
> precisely that data as a byproduct of work people were going to do
> anyway.

**Where the confidence stops.** Read at full text, extracted locally
from the Cornell PDF. Sections read in detail are the abstract, 3.1,
3.2, 5, 5.1, part of 5.2, 6 and the Impact Statement. Sections 2, 4 and
5.3 and the appendices were not read, so this brief makes no claim
about what the paper does or does not say on topics it does not raise
in those sections. In particular the α-informative condition assumes a
single fixed R* per context, and the sections read model noise but not
a change in R* itself, which is a statement about the sections read and
not about the paper.

### 2. The nearest published neighbour to Ursa Major exists, it is eight months old, and on inspection it does neither of the two things Ursa treats as non-negotiable (high confidence, full text partly read)

**[E9] Maeda, Sekiya, Sugimura, Asai, Tsuda, Ikeda, Fujii and Watanabe,
*Puda: Private User Dataset Agent for User-Sovereign and
Privacy-Preserving Personalized AI*** (arXiv:2602.08268, v1 9 February
2026, v2 10 February 2026, cs.AI). **Nine runs of this seat's prior-art
sweep did not find this paper**, and it is the closest thing to Ursa
Major's architecture in the published record.

**What it shares with Ursa Major.** It aggregates a user's personal
data across services and lets the user manage it, and it offers three
disclosure levels, which are detailed browsing history, extracted
keywords, and predefined category subsets. It reports that **predefined
category subsets achieved 97.2% of the personalization performance of
sharing detailed browsing history.** That is an external, measured
statement that coarse derived signal retains nearly all the
personalization value of raw particulars, which is the price analog in
`docs/vision.md` §0b stated as an experiment by somebody with no stake
in Ursa. It is the first such measurement this seat has found.

**What it does not do, quoted from the full text.** Despite the word
sovereign in the title, **"The collected data is transmitted to the
server hosting the Dataset Agent"**, and the paper's own limitations
note that "employing a cloud-based model introduces a limitation". The
Dataset Agent and the Access Control Agent "may be operated by distinct
administrative entities", so the user's data sits with operators rather
than on the user's machine. And the consent layer is unbuilt, in the
paper's own words: **"the authorization mechanism is limited to the
design phase"**, with retrieval permitted "strictly within the scope
permitted by the user's authorization" as the only operative statement.

**So Ursa Major's architectural claim survives, and it now survives
against a named competitor rather than against an empty territory.**
`CLAUDE.md`'s load-bearing constraint 3 is that raw processing happens
on-device and raw data never touches the aggregation layer. Puda
transmits the raw collected data to a server. Constraint 2 is that the
user can see, edit, revoke and delete. Puda's authorization mechanism
is a design. The nearest neighbour claims user sovereignty in its title
and implements neither property, which is a far better differentiation
story than an absence.

**The 97.2% figure is reported, not endorsed, and the paper tells you
why.** Scoring is by judge: "Gemini 2.5 Flash was employed as the judge
LLM", on a five-point scale across Abstracted Preference Match,
Serendipity and Groundedness. Its own preliminary study found
"inter-rater reliability for single humans to be moderate" and
"Correlation between LLM and human averages was positive but moderate",
and it names "a distinct gap was observed in the Groundedness metric".
The dataset is largely synthetic, "generated by 20 fictional personas",
on a single travel-planning task. Quote the architecture finding
freely. Quote the number only with the judge and the synthetic personas
attached.

**Where the confidence stops.** The HTML full text is 121,456
characters and this run read the first 100,000. A neutral prompt asking
for sentences bearing on deletion and revocation returned none from
that span, which is a statement about the first 100,000 characters and
not about the paper. The final 21,456 characters are unread and are the
first thing to read next run.
