## Where this period is heading

**The field has started measuring how much of a judge's score is not
about the work, and the answer keeps coming back large.**

The nineteenth run reached this from one direction and said so, naming
computer vision as the honest limit on it. This run found the same
result arriving from three more directions, in two more arXiv
categories, across five months, from four groups with no connection to
each other or to Ursa.

- A **rubric-only classifier**, given no access to the candidate
  response at all, predicts an LLM judge's output above chance, and
  judges often fail to update when either the response or the rubric
  criterion is reversed [E190]. Text, cs.CL, 31 August 2026.
- A **nine-judge panel** carries about two independent votes' worth of
  information, falls 8 to 22 points short of independent voting, and is
  matched or beaten by its own best single member [E191]. Text, cs.CL,
  28 May 2026.
- A **counterfactual grounding score** is provably capped by whether the
  edit reached the judge at all, so its error runs one way only [E187].
  Vision, cs.CV, 9 September 2026.
- **Agreement among forecasters** does not survive separation from the
  predictability of the asset and from the aggregation rule used
  [E192]. Finance, cs.MA, 9 October 2026.

Four measurements, four domains, one shape. The quantity labs use to
decide whether an open-ended output is good is substantially a property
of the instrument rather than of the output.

**The part that matters commercially is what the field reaches for
next.** The standard remedy is more judges and better aggregation, and
[E191] closes that door with a number. Established aggregation methods
recover at most 11 per cent of the gap, and they do not recover it even
when handed the correct answers. The bottleneck is that the judges make
the same mistakes on the same items, so adding judges adds correlated
error rather than information. In the authors' own words, scaling up
panels cannot substitute for genuinely independent evaluation.

**That sentence is Ursa Minor's pitch, written by someone else.** Ursa's
artifact label is not a judge at all, and it is not a panel of judges
either. It is what the work kept. Whatever else is wrong with it, it
cannot be correlated with the errors of a judge panel, because no judge
is in the loop. Until now this seat has been able to say that preference
proxies grade appearance. It can now say something stronger and
narrower, which is that the field's own fix for that problem is
measured and capped, and the cap is not a matter of engineering effort.

**What a builder should do differently this week.** Nothing in Ursa's
code changes on this evidence. One thing in Ursa's reporting might, and
it is written up under the engineer section rather than asserted here,
because it rests on abstracts.

## What is RISING

### 1. The judge-score-is-partly-not-about-the-work result has crossed from vision into text

**Medium-high confidence. Both sources read at abstract depth, and this
is stated rather than buried, because the nineteenth run's equivalent
claim rested on a full text and this one does not.**

"Judging LLM-as-a-Judge: Concerning Rubric Artifacts in LLM-based
Automated Text Generation Evaluation" [E190] reports two things. First,
classifiers trained only on rubric text, with no access to any evaluated
response, achieve nontrivial predictive performance on judge outputs, so
scores can be partly anticipated independently of what was judged.
Second, counterfactual perturbations show that judges often fail to
reliably update their decisions when either the candidate response or
the rubric criterion is reversed.

The second half is the same mechanism as [E187]'s, which is that the
verdict does not follow the edit. One was found in computer vision and
announced in October. The other is text, it is cs.CL, and it was
submitted on 31 August 2026, four weeks earlier. Neither cites the
other in anything this run read.

**Why this is the finding and not a footnote.** The nineteenth run named
transfer as the open limit on its own synthesis, saying the mechanism
transfers and the measurement does not, and that Ursa sells writing,
research and applied engineering rather than vision. The transfer is no
longer a hypothesis. It is a separate paper in the domain Ursa actually
sells into, obtained independently, reaching the same shape.

**The honest limit.** This is an abstract-page read of both the rubric
claim and the perturbation claim. No number from [E190] is quoted here,
because "nontrivial predictive performance" is what the abstract says
and a figure would need the full text. A later run that wants to put a
number on the text side should open arXiv:2609.02942 in full.

### 2. Panel agreement is mostly correlated error, which caps the remedy the field is reaching for

**High confidence on what is claimed, which is what the abstract states.
Abstract depth, full text not read.**

"Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM
Evaluation Panels" [E191] tests nine frontier judges from seven model
families on three natural-language-inference datasets, each with 100
human annotations per item. The panel supplies about two independent
votes' worth of information. Roughly three quarters of its nominal
independence is lost because the models err on the same items. Panel
accuracy falls 8 to 22 percentage points short of what independent
voting would deliver, and the best single judge matches or outperforms
the full panel in every condition tested. Established aggregation
methods close at most 11 per cent of the gap even with access to the
correct answers. The authors quantify this with Kish effective sample
size and a Condorcet null model, and report it robust across prompt
variants, temperatures, chain-of-thought and a pairwise preference task
on RewardBench.

Two independent results point the same way. "When the Judge Acts" [E188]
finds that an agreement filter helps only when it targets the judge's
own failure mode, and that for a stronger 8B judge the same filter
mostly discards good decisions, so filters must be re-audited whenever
the judge changes. "When AI Advisors Agree" [E192] separates agreement
from asset predictability and from aggregation-specific skill across 928
quarterly outcomes on 232 assets, finds the apparent value of directional
agreement disappears under modal direction scoring, and concludes that
agreement, visible risk and aggregation-specific skill should be
evaluated separately before consensus is presented as confidence.

**Why it is Ursa's.** `CLAUDE.md` §4 sells cross-model comparison as one
of three properties nobody else offers. The obvious objection a lab
raises is that it already compares models by polling several of them.
[E191] is the measured answer to that objection and it did not come from
Ursa.

### 3. The one-sided-error result, now at full text rather than abstract

**High confidence on the construction, which was read in the full text
this run. This upgrades the nineteenth run's finding 2 rather than
restating it.**

"A Low Grounding Score Is Not an Ungrounded Judge" [E187] proves a
reading bound, that the Verdict Grounding Score is capped by the rate at
which an edit reaches the judge's decision-relevant reading, which the
paper calls the perceptibility confound. Its Theorem 5 then argues the
missing quantity is not merely bounded but identified, from three
measurements that an auditor has already collected, so the correction
costs no new data. The error is one-sided, because the counterfactual
probe never overstates the judge's read accuracy on the unedited image.
It ships GroundJudge, a label-free diagnostic needing no human
annotation. The ordering its Corollary 6 predicts held strictly in all
18 primary cells, with a mean shortfall of 0.32 and a permutation test
at p below 5 x 10⁻⁵, and the typical judge acted on only about half the
edits it could otherwise read. Six false-alarm cases were identified.
The paper states that blank-image ablation measures image presence
rather than grounding, that a counterfactual grounding score should
never be reported alone, and that the characteristic failure is retiring
a judge that reads the image.

Its limitations are stated plainly: the injected errors are synthetic,
the primary pool is two datasets, the headline rate lacks per-cell
intervals, and the audit is retrodiction, with one held-out test
confirming predictions.

## Top extraction targets for the skill agent

**One proposed this run.**

### Target 1 — reading a score that is about your instrument rather than about the work

**Rank 1. Four mutually reinforcing sources across three domains, one
read at full text.**

The procedure, stated as the sources state it. When an attribution,
grounding or agreement score is used to decide whether an instrument can
be trusted, never report it alone. Pair it with the cheap measurement
that upper-bounds it, because the gap between the two is the part of the
score that is about the probe rather than about the instrument. Know the
direction of the error before acting on the score, since a one-sided
error means the characteristic failure is discarding something that
works [E187]. And re-audit the pairing whenever the instrument changes,
because a filter tuned to one failure mode discards good decisions under
another [E188].

The judgment it carries. When agreement between two checkers is the
evidence, establish how correlated their errors are before treating
agreement as confirmation, because nine judges can be worth two [E191],
and agreement has to be separated from the predictability of the thing
being judged and from the aggregation rule before it is presented as
confidence [E192].

**Where it applies in Ursa.** The span resolver produces attributions,
and `generated_deleted` and `no_generation_provenance` are both the
output of a bounded check over an artifact rather than observations. The
existing skill `adjudicating-uncertain-spans` decides span labels and
says nothing about the direction of error in the score behind them
[E196].

**The honest overlap, which narrows the gap and does not close it.**
`skills/quoting-a-number-from-an-outcome-record` already reasons in
bound-direction terms in at least three places, including the statement
that an adjudicated `no_generation_provenance` share is an upper bound.
So the library already thinks this way about its own statistics. What it
has nowhere is the step before that, which is what to do when the number
in hand is a score about an instrument and a second cheap probe would
bound it. The skill agent is free to judge that the gap is too small to
justify a file, and that judgment is its own.

**Why it clears the bar now when the nineteenth run said it did not.**
That run had one source at abstract depth. This run has [E187] at full
text with its theorem, its ordering result and its stated rule, plus
[E190] reaching the same mechanism in text, plus [E191] and [E192] on
the agreement half from two unrelated fields. The charter's bar is
several mutually reinforcing sources on one technique, and the vision-only
objection that run raised against itself is answered by [E190] and
[E191] being cs.CL.

**Not proposed, and named so it is not rediscovered.** The assurance
ledger's typed-claim taxonomy from [E193] is not re-proposed, because
the 2026-10-16 brief already proposed it and
`adding-a-bound-to-the-record-gate` already decides whether a rule is a
bound or a measurement.
