## What is rising

### 1. The artifact-side label is not falsified, but its novelty is: learning from user edits now has published theory with bounds (high confidence)

[F1] **Misra, Pacchiano, Chi and Gao, *Principled Fine-tuning of LLMs
from User-Edits: A Medley of Preference, Supervision, and Reward*** —
**NeurIPS 2025**, arXiv 2601.19055. It studies fine-tuning "using
user-edit deployment data consisting of a set of context, an agent's
response, and user edits", and says the quiet part in the abstract:
"The *natural* origin of user edits makes it a desired source for
adapting and personalizing LLMs." It proves bounds for learning from
each of preference, supervised label and cost, shows the three trade
off against each other, and ensembles them.

That is `docs/beyond-preference-pairs.md` §3.2 with theorems attached,
and it is a stronger hit than the prior-art the last brief found,
because it is not an adjacent domain — it is this exact signal, named
as deployment data from writing assistants and coding agents.

[F2] **Gao, Taymanov, Salinas, Mineiro and Misra, *Aligning LLM Agents
by Learning Latent Preference from User Edits*** (PRELUDE/CIPHER,
arXiv 2404.15269, Apr 2024) is the paper [F1] builds on, and the last
brief listed it on the watchlist as **not opened**. Opening it took
four minutes and it carries the successor. That is the second
consecutive run where the deferred item was the load-bearing one.

**Now the asset, and it is bigger than the hit.** Both papers evaluate
on "two interactive environments — summarization and email writing" with
**a GPT-4 simulated user**. [F1] inherits those same two domains from
[F2]. The field has the algorithms, the bounds and the ensembling
procedure for learning from user edits. **What it does not have is a
single real one.** It simulates the user, because nobody can get the
edits.

This changes what Ursa Minor should say, and it is a better sentence
than the one it replaces:

> The method of learning from corrections is published, peer-reviewed
> and sitting in your pipeline's reach. The corpus it needs does not
> exist, which is why the state of the art evaluates it against a GPT-4
> impersonating a user across two toy domains. Ursa produces that
> corpus from work nobody was paid to label.

A claim of *novel method* in front of a post-training researcher who
knows [F1] spends credibility. A claim of *the missing corpus for a
method you already trust* spends none, and it is the claim the evidence
actually supports. The previous brief's narrowing sequence gains a row:
the mechanism is published; the artifact-side label at population scale
is still standing.

### 2. Third-party confirmation that repository provenance dies in exactly the git operations the engineer seat has been hardening against (high confidence)

[F3] **Robbes, Matricon, Degueule, Hora and Zacchiroli, *Promises,
Perils, and (Timely) Heuristics for Mining Coding Agent Activity***
(arXiv 2601.18345, Jan 2026) documents eight perils of mining coding
agent activity on GitHub. Three of them are Ursa's own engineering
backlog, found independently by people with no stake in it: **squash
merges** destroying individual commit identity, **rebases and force
pushes** rewriting the metadata away, and **co-author trailers** being
optional and unreliable as a fallback. Its recommended heuristics are
all *external* — branch-name conventions, agent config files, PR labels,
commit-message strings.

Read that as the competitive statement it is. The published state of the
art for knowing what an agent produced in a repository is **guessing
from branch names after the fact**, and its authors say plainly where
the guess breaks. Ursa does not guess: it records the generation at
launch and resolves provenance forward. The engineer seat's merge-
attribution work and the skill seat's squash-merge label inversion are
the same defect this paper names, and they now have an outside citation
that says the retrospective route cannot close it.
