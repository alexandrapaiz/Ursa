## What is rising

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by attention.

### 1. Ursa Major's claim has prior art, it is open source, and in eight runs this seat never looked (high confidence, two primaries read)

This is the finding of the week and it is a finding against ourselves.

**[E1] Ravindran, *Portable Agent Memory: A Protocol for
Cryptographically-Verified Memory Transfer Across Heterogeneous AI
Agents*** (arXiv:2605.11032, 10 May 2026, cs.CR). An **open protocol
with an Apache-2.0 reference implementation**: a five-component memory
model covering episodic, semantic, procedural, working-state and
**identity preferences**, entries content-addressed and linked in a
Merkle-DAG provenance graph for tamper-evidence, capability-based access
control for scoped disclosure, an injection-resistant rehydration step,
a JSON-first wire format, a Python SDK with 54 passing tests, and
**cross-model transfer demos across GPT-4, Claude, Gemini and Llama.**
Its framing sentence is Ursa Major's problem statement: agent context
"remains locked within vendor-specific runtimes."

**[E2] Lin and seventeen co-authors, *LLM Agents Enable User-Governed
Personalization Beyond Platform Boundaries*** (arXiv:2605.09794, 10 May
2026, cs.IR). A position paper arguing the exact shift Ursa Major is.
"Personalization today is fundamentally platform-centric." "The key
asymmetry lies in data access: only users can aggregate their own
cross-platform and offline information." It reports proof-of-concept
evidence that "users equipped with cross-platform data exports and an
off-the-shelf LLM agent can outperform" single-platform baselines.

**Read the bad news first, because it is the useful half.** Every brief
this seat has written searched prior art for Ursa **Minor**: outcome
records, survival of generated text, learning from user edits, bandit
feedback. Eight runs. The searches were good and two of them landed
real corrections. **None of them was ever pointed at Ursa Major**, which
`CLAUDE.md` §2 names as the primary asset, the one that "compounds daily
and can be destroyed in a week." It took one search to find an open
protocol and an eighteen-author position paper sitting on the consumer
product's central claim, both published 10 May 2026, both five months
old before this seat read either.

**Now the differentiation, and it narrows to something better than
portability.** [E1] carries identity preferences across four vendors'
models today, with cryptographic tamper-evidence Ursa does not have. So
*portability is not the moat and should stop being pitched as one.* What
[E1] does not have, checked against the primary and stated as absence
rather than as weakness:

- **No user.** No pilot size, no participants, no evaluation results of
  any kind. Transfer is demonstrated, never measured.
- **No consent, ownership or revocation surface.** Capability-based
  scoped access control is an engineering primitive for an integrator.
  It is not the user's right to see, edit, revoke and delete, which is
  `CLAUDE.md`'s non-negotiable #2.
- **No revealed preference.** It transports a memory state that some
  upstream system already wrote. Nothing in it derives preferences from
  real work, which is the whole of Ursa's first principle.

And [E2], which is the sharper of the two, **argues Ursa Major's
strategic case better than Ursa's own documents do** and should simply
be adopted. Its asymmetry claim is the answer to "why can't a lab just
build this": not that labs lack the skill, but that no platform can
aggregate what only the user can reach. That is a third party's framing
of `CLAUDE.md` §5's central strategic problem, and it is citable.

So the honest Major sentence, which is narrower and sturdier than the
one in the record:

> Moving a profile between vendors is a solved engineering problem with
> an Apache-2.0 implementation. What nobody has built is the profile a
> user owns, derived from work they were already doing, that they can
> audit and revoke. The transport is commodity. The consent and the
> derivation are the product.

### 2. Multi-modal, finally covered rather than listed, and the field's instruments fail Ursa's own principles there too (medium confidence, two primaries read, two legs listing-level)

Three runs of covered-by-listing is now one run of covered. The
territory is dense, and the pattern across it is one thing.

**[E3] Gao, Dai, Guan, Gao, Wu, Shen and Lau, *Personalization Matters:
Long-Horizon Conversation Agent with User-Centric Information in Online
Shopping Interactions*** (arXiv:2610.11375, **8 October 2026**, cs.MA,
accepted to AACL-IJCNLP 2026). The only paper in this run's sweep from
the actual window. A multi-agent multimodal RAG system, and more
interesting for Ursa, **a trajectory-level evaluation protocol** on four
dimensions: Global Preference Consistency, Cumulative Information
Synthesis, Interaction Trajectory and Tone Consistency. That is
independent movement toward the trajectory metadata `CLAUDE.md` §1
wraps around the outcome record, named in a different vocabulary.

**[E5] APeB** (arXiv:2607.03162, 3 Jul 2026, revised 27 Aug 2026) is the
closest benchmark to Ursa Major's job that exists. It is built "from
action logs", pairs "underspecified intents with histories" and reports
that models "handle explicit queries well but struggle with early-stage
queries requiring intent and preference discovery". Preferences are not
represented at all. They are left implicit in raw histories the agent
must mine. That is Ursa Major's problem posed as a benchmark by someone
else.

**The pattern, and it is the part worth carrying.** Across this
territory the measuring instrument is always one of three things: a
reference answer, a forced choice among curated candidates, or a judge.
[E3]'s automatic trajectory metrics carry the weight of its result
(4.82 against 3.74) while its human leg is **five participants**, which
its own authors call exploratory. [E5] scores agents choosing among
"user-viewed candidate items". [E7]'s benchmarks are question-answering
against references or two-image forced choice. Read against
`docs/vision.md` §0b this is the second principle and the fourth,
failing in the multi-modal half exactly as the 2026-10-06 brief found
them failing in the text half: a benchmark that demands the spec up
front contradicts discovery, and a metric the system conforms to
contradicts the pretence principle. **Ursa's "missing verifier for
open-ended work" claim now extends to multi-modal with the same
evidence shape.** That is a genuine expansion of the thesis's reach and
it cost one run of coverage.

**Where the confidence comes from, precisely.** [E3] and [E5] were
opened and read. The two legs of the pattern that rest on **[E6]** (GDP,
CVPR 2026, whose PDF returned 403) and **[E7]** (PerVL-Bench,
SocialPersona, PrefGen) are **listing-level**. The pattern is therefore
medium confidence, not high, and the next run can lift it by opening
[E6] through a route that is not the CVF PDF endpoint. [E6] is also the
one unretired prior-art risk in this area: it is described as the first
multimodal personalization benchmark centred on **dynamic** user
preference modelling, which is adjacent to Ursa Major, and this run
could not verify that description against the paper.

### 3. The deferred Heidelberg paper, closed, and it inverts in Ursa's favour (high confidence, primary read)

**[E4] Kreutzer, Berger and Riezler, *Correct Me If You Can: Learning
from Error Corrections and Markings*** (EAMT 2020, arXiv:2004.11222).
Listed unopened by the 2026-10-06 brief. Opened, and **retired from the
watchlist rather than carried for a fourth run.**

It is a user study of the cost-versus-signal tradeoff in exactly Ursa's
mechanism. Its finding: error marking, where a human flags wrong spans
instead of rewriting them, "enables precise credit assignment" at
"significantly less human effort than correcting/post-editing", and
models fine-tune successfully on real human markings rather than the
simulated markings earlier work relied on.

**The inversion is the point.** The field's documented reason for
preferring markings over edits is that **post-editing is too expensive
to collect.** Ursa's `survived_mutated` class is post-editing at zero
annotation cost, because nobody was annotating. The strongest form of
the claim is therefore not that edits are a better signal than markings,
which [E4] does not support. It is that the literature chose the weaker
signal for a cost reason Ursa's capture method removes. That is a
cleaner argument than the record currently makes, it is eight years
old, and it is free.

Note also the shape, now three for three: this paper, [E1] and [E2] were
all reachable from items a prior brief listed and did not open. **Fourth
consecutive run where a deferred item was load-bearing.** The 2026-10-06
brief's lesson that a named-but-unopened watchlist item is not coverage
is confirmed again, and the meta-review's subject is its dual.
