## Method and confidence, stated first

**There is no corpus behind this brief.** `NEON_RO_URL` is unset in
this runner, and the larger point is that nothing sits behind it.
Ursa has no research pipeline, no digest, no claims database, and no
`sources.yaml`. The skill agent reached the same conclusion on its
own first run and filed it as a finding (PR #14, "Ursa has no claims
database, so the skill seat cannot extract"). This brief corroborates
that finding from a second seat.

So the charter's ranking instruments are all unavailable this week.
There is no citation velocity from `citation_log`, no embedding-space
novelty from the `discovery_report` queries, and no supports or
contradicts edges to audit. What replaces them is a live-web
ecosystem check against the owner-named coverage areas, which is what
`docs/standards/lessons.md` L-R1 requires of any seat owning a live
territory, every run. Ursa's copy of the research charter does not
carry that check. Adding it is this week's meta-review proposal.

Confidence is therefore stated per item rather than assumed. Every
claim below carries an evidence ref with a date, and every ref was
opened and read rather than taken from a search snippet. Where a
source turned out to overstate something, the brief says so.

## What is rising

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by how much attention it is
getting.

### 1. Learned judges are losing their claim to independence (high confidence)

The strongest external result of the period for Ursa's thesis.
**[E1]** is a survey of 259 works on agentic artifact creation,
published 2026-08-28, covering 230 systems and 29 benchmarks through
2026-08-20. Its evaluation finding, in its own words: *"Learned judges
may add little independent evidence when they share the generator's
preferences or blind spots."*

That sentence is Ursa Minor's commercial premise, arrived at
independently and at survey scale. CLAUDE.md §5 states it as "Ursa
Minor is not selling more preference pairs into a market that already
has them. It's selling the missing verifier for open-ended work."
Until now that was our assertion. **[E1]** makes it a citable position
in the literature, from authors with no stake in Ursa.

**[E2]** supplies the measured version for one concrete domain. It
audits 150 failure-scored trajectories across five computer-use
benchmarks and finds **15.3% of FAIL verdicts are wrong**, split into
10.7% evaluator false negatives and 4.7% broken tasks. It also finds
that a single scalar success rate cannot explain the genuine failures,
which fall mostly into verification and planning rather than execution.

The two together say the grader is unreliable and the scalar hides the
cause. Ursa's answer to both is the same object: the record is
supplied by the artifact, and the trajectory is kept rather than
collapsed into a number.

**[E2] is also the sharpest external statement of the fourth principle
of intelligence** (docs/vision.md §0b, "Systems built on them conform
to the measure, not the world"). It is the first time that principle
has a measured number attached to it from outside this repo.

### 2. Architecture beats model choice in multi-agent systems (high confidence)

**[E3]**, revised 2026-09-17, fixes the underlying LLM and varies only
architecture across nine frameworks. Orchestration alone costs over
60x latency. Schema-constrained planning interfaces cost up to 32
accuracy points through formatting failures rather than reasoning
errors. Mismatched communication topology drops coordination success
from above 90% to below 30%. Memory architecture governs recall
independently of context window size.

This is a standing coverage area the owner named, and it is the one
where the evidence moved most this period.

One line in **[E3]** applies directly to this repo rather than to a
product: *"no evaluated framework natively supports controlled
knowledge revision."* Ursa's seat roster is a multi-agent system whose
knowledge lives in charters, and this run is a knowledge-revision
proposal against a charter. The gap **[E3]** names in the field is the
gap the meta-review loop exists to close here.

### 3. Process-and-outcome evaluation is becoming its own category (medium confidence)

Multi-modal, the third owner-named area, is where this is clearest.
**[E4]** benchmarks multi-modal deep research agents on process and
outcome as separate dimensions. **[E5]** is process-verified, with
over 2,000 stepwise checkpoints across 418 tasks.

Medium rather than high because the reading here rests on abstracts
and summaries rather than on full texts, and because two papers is a
direction rather than a consensus. Worth watching, not yet worth
building on.

The relevance is that the field is converging on the same shape Ursa
already ships. `outcome_record.json` carries span outcomes and
`trajectory.md` carries the process. The difference is that these
benchmarks verify process against a curated checkpoint list, which
requires specifying the result beforehand, and the second principle of
intelligence says that leaves nothing to discover. Ursa's process
signal is recovered from real work instead. That contrast is a
paragraph the methods doc should carry.

### 4. Memory layers are consolidating, and the labs are shipping their own (high confidence on the dates, medium on the read)

Google shipped persistent custom instructions for Gemini across Drive,
Chat, Gmail, Sheets and Slides beginning **2026-09-02** **[E6]**, and
conversation history to the Workspace side panel earlier in the year
**[E7]**. A dedicated third-party memory layer category has formed
around Mem0, Zep and similar products.

**A correction worth recording.** A secondary blog claimed Google
integrated persistent personalization across Gemini in January 2026.
Google's own Workspace Updates posts do not support that date. The
custom-instructions rollout is 2026-09-02 **[E6]** and the
conversation-history rollout is staged through March and April 2026
**[E7]**. The brief uses the primary source. This is the kind of thing
the charter's "say plainly when something over-claims" rule is for,
and it applies to the research seat's own inputs too.

The read, at medium confidence: every lab shipping sticky per-product
memory raises the cost of Ursa Major's portability promise and moves
the central strategic problem in CLAUDE.md §5 from theory to the
present tense. The labs are simultaneously the platform, the customer,
and the party most able to shut Ursa down, and they are now building
the retention half of the thing Ursa offers. It does not invalidate
the thesis, because per-product memory is the opposite of portable and
is not consented, cross-model, or revealed-preference. It does mean
the landscape doc cannot treat this category as adjacent any more.

## What the engineer should know before building

- **[E3]**'s schema-constrained-planning result is the one to act on.
  Up to 32 accuracy points lost to formatting failures rather than
  reasoning errors is a warning about over-constraining structured
  output. Relevant wherever `ursa-major/src` parses model output into
  typed shapes, and relevant to the `artifact.kind` work in PR #16.
- **[E2]**'s 10.7% evaluator-false-negative rate is the number to keep
  in mind while retuning the match thresholds in
  `ursa-major/src/match.ts` for KR1.1. A resolver that adjudicates
  spans is an evaluator, and evaluators in this literature fail by
  rejecting valid alternatives. The KR's 10% false-positive target has
  a false-negative twin that the KR does not currently name.
- Nothing in this week's evidence changes the resolver v2 design in
  `trial/task-001/trajectory.md`. Build as specified.

## What looked hot but is noise

- **"LLM-as-a-judge best practices" content.** High volume, almost all
  of it vendor blogs recycling the same guidance. The one real result
  in this space this period is **[E1]**'s finding that shared blind
  spots undercut judge independence, and that is a survey paper, not
  the blog wave around it.
- **RLHF-alternative acronym churn.** DPO, GRPO, RLAIF, PAR, PaTaRM
  and similar all optimize how a preference signal is consumed. None
  of them changes where the signal comes from, which is the only axis
  Ursa competes on. Not worth a skill and not worth a sprint item.
- **Generic "AI agents in 2026" survey content.** Large volume, no
  measurement. **[E3]** is the counterexample that makes the rest
  skippable, because it actually controlled for the model.
