## What is rising

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by attention.

### 1. The market stopped waiting for a verifier and built a synthetic one (high confidence)

This is the most important external development for Ursa Minor since
the seat began reading, and it changes how the pitch has to be stated.

`CLAUDE.md` §5 says Ursa Minor "is selling the missing verifier for
open-ended work," and `docs/research/minor-training-fit.md` §1 calls
the RLVR row "the missing verifier for open-ended work: declared
satisfaction plus the survival scalar." Between February and September
2026 the field filled that slot with **rubrics**, and it did so at
volume. **[E9]** lists eight papers in eight months building
rubric-based reward for open-ended post-training, and **[E3]** states
the position plainly: "Rubrics have emerged as an alternative to RLVR
in open-ended domains where a single ground-truth final answer is not
available."

By September the loop had closed on itself. **[E1]**, posted
2026-09-01, opens with "Training open-ended agents via reinforcement
learning (RL) is hindered by the lack of verifiable gold answers and
scalable rubrics," and its answer is to have the model generate the
tasks and the rubrics it is then scored against, co-evolving generator
and solver. The grader and the graded are now the same weights.

**[E2]** is the result that matters most, because it measures what the
substitute costs. Optimizing against a rubric verifier and evaluating
against a cross-family panel of three frontier judges, it separates
verifier failure from rubric-design limitation and finds that "even
strong rubric-based verifiers favor responses that rubric-free judges
rate worse overall," that "weak verifiers produce large proxy-reward
gains that do not transfer," that exploitation grows over training, and
that stronger verifiers "substantially reduce, but do not eliminate"
it. The named recurring failures are partial satisfaction of compound
criteria, treating implicit content as explicit, and imprecise topical
matching.

**What this means for us, concretely.** The competitive claim can no
longer be absence, because the market's own literature says it has an
instrument. The claim is provenance. A rubric is a specification
written before the work, so optimizing against it is the fourth
principle of intelligence with a mechanism and a measurement attached:
systems conform to the measure rather than the world, and **[E2]** is
the arithmetic. An outcome record carries no criteria at all. Nobody
wrote down what good looked like, the work either used the text or
did not, and that label cannot be satisfied partially or matched
topically because it is not a criterion.

`docs/research/minor-training-fit.md` §1 should carry this. Its fourth
row is currently written against a gap, and the gap now has an
incumbent occupant. One paragraph, citing **[E1]** and **[E2]**, turns
the weakest sentence in the methods doc into its strongest.

### 2. Auditing is separating from scoring, and the audit looks like provenance (high confidence)

**[E4]** was revised 2026-09-28, inside this window. Its title is the
thesis: *Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery*. It proposes a
certification protocol that "distinguishes achieved utility from
process repeatability," and its Gate 2 asks whether matched agents can
recover a result using only the starting information and observed
content, with run history and new measurements excluded. A result that
is recoverable without the run was not produced by the run.

That is structurally Ursa's `no_generation_provenance` test with the
sign flipped. Ursa asks which spans of a finished work trace to no
generation at all, meaning the model was never in the running.
**[E4]** asks whether a claimed improvement traces to the process that
claimed it. Both refuse to accept a scalar as evidence of a
contribution, and both answer by joining the outcome backward to what
produced it.

Worth acting on because it supplies peer-reviewed vocabulary for
KR2.2's auditability story, which currently argues from first
principles alone.

### 3. A frontier lab shipped cross-model portability, and this seat missed it twice (high confidence, and a detection failure)

**[E5]** is Anthropic's own help-center article. Claude accepts memory
imported from another AI provider and exports its own memory, on Free,
Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise, on web and desktop. **[E6]**, the Data
Transfer Initiative's 2026-09-01 post, names it alongside Gemini import
guidance as the state of the art and says the remaining gap is imports.

Ursa Major's value proposition in `CLAUDE.md` §3 reads "Your AI history
and preferences, portable across every model you use," and "When you
leave a model, your relationship with AI doesn't reset." A lab now
ships that sentence. The 2026-09-25 brief read the retention half of
this trend, which was Google's persistent custom instructions, and
missed the portability half entirely. Under L-R1 owner-reported news
about your own declared territory is a detection failure rather than an
input, and this is the same failure class caught internally on the
third run instead of by the owner. It is filed as such.

**The read, and it is better news than it first looks.** The mechanism
in **[E5]** is copy-pasted free text. There is no file format and no
schema. The export is produced by asking the donor model to "Write out
your memories of me verbatim," so the payload is prose one model wrote
about you. Anthropic marks the feature "experimental and still in
active development" and warns that "Claude may not always successfully
incorporate imported memories," and that it prioritizes work-related
content. So what shipped is the promise, not the fidelity.

Two consequences, both actionable now.

First, Ursa Major's differentiation moves from *portability exists
here* to *portability with structure, evidence and revocation*.
`ursa-major/src/tuning/types.ts` already makes the distinction the
product needs: every axiom carries `evidence` pointers joinable back to
record steps, `evidenceCount` is code-owned arithmetic rather than a
model's self-report, `basis` separates stated from tacit, and
revocation survives re-distills. **[E5]** has none of that by
construction, because prose cannot carry a pointer.

Second, and this is the part to act on this week:
`ursa-major/src/tuning/export.ts` already emits exactly the artifact
**[E5]** consumes. `renderTuningBlock` renders a paste-in context
block, and **[E5]** says "Paste the exported text from your previous AI
provider into the text box and click the 'Add to memory' button." Ursa
can demonstrate end-to-end portability into a frontier product today,
with no new code, and the demonstration is a screenshot rather than an
argument.

**And it passes the §5 test.** `CLAUDE.md` §5 asks of every decision
whether it makes Ursa more valuable to partner with than to fight. A
lab using memory import as a switching funnel wants the highest
fidelity source format it can get, because the quality of the import is
what makes the switch stick. A structured, evidence-backed, revocable
tuning record is the best possible input to that funnel. This trend
moves Ursa toward the partner side of the test, not away from it.

### 5. Multi-modal memory is being benchmarked, and the results are unflattering (medium confidence)

**[E7]** benchmarks multimodal conversational memory on realistic
user-agent interaction and reports "persistent gaps in cross-modal
grounding, cross session reasoning, and the efficiency cost of
accumulating multimodal context." Its own method improves accuracy by
13% while cutting index-construction time and retrieved tokens by over
70%.

Medium confidence because the paper is from 2026-06-05 and only its
abstract was read. It covers the owner's multi-modal area with a memory
angle, which is the intersection Ursa Major sits in, and the efficiency
number is the interesting half: the cost of accumulated context is
being treated as a first-class problem, which is an argument for a
distilled tuning record over a growing transcript.

## What looked hot but is noise

- **The rubric-RL acronym wave.** **[E9]** is eight papers in eight
  months with names like RISE-RL, EvoRubric, Focal Reward and ARISE-RL,
  and most of them vary how a rubric is generated, weighted or
  distilled. Two are load-bearing: **[E2]**, which measures the
  failure, and **[E1]**, which closes the loop by having the model
  write its own rubrics. The rest can be skipped without loss, and
  tracking the acronyms would cost a run a week for no decision.
- **"AI agent security in 2026" content.** High volume, near-total
  recycling, and actively harmful as an input. The underlying Wiz and
  Trend Micro research is real. The blog layer around it is not, and
  one page in it invented a CVE this run.
- **RLHF-alternative acronym churn**, carried from PR #19 and still
  true. DPO, GRPO, KTO and kin all optimize how a preference signal is
  consumed. None changes where the signal comes from, which remains the
  only axis Ursa competes on.
