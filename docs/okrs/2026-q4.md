*Principles check:* no violation found. The trace-stage detector (PR
#13) explicitly declines to name the mechanism behind a correction
("a language judgment... belongs to the distiller downstream, not the
detector"), which is the opposite of a central grader and the right
call under Distributed Intelligence. The verdict reader
(`ursa-major/src/verdict.ts`, already on `main`) recognizes only a
stated verdict and treats silence as `undeclared`, never inferred from
retention. That is the Tacit Intelligence principle honored in code,
not violated. KR1.1's threshold retuning calibrates the matcher's own
accuracy against ground truth. It does not make the system conform its
behavior to a KPI, so it does not trip the Pretence principle either.

*Tiebreak check:* no manual intervention substituted for system
capability this month. If anything the opposite happened, and that is
the month's real finding: the thing missing is the owner's or a
delegated agent's merge, not agent effort. The mitigation already in
motion is itself autonomy-increasing and worth naming rather than
re-discovering. HQ decision 041 gives the PM Tier B merge authority
under six written conditions, vendored here as PR #44
(`chair/pm-merges`, still open), and the PM's own 2026-09-30 standup
(PR #45) and the exo seat's 2026-09-27 audit (PR #30, "nothing failed,
everything jammed") already surfaced the queue collapse in detail. This
check-in adds one thing those did not have yet: the quarter-level cost
in KR terms, above, and the observation that the backlog is not merely
stale. It fed a regression, KR1.3 going from n=1 to n=0.
